Finding Hospice Care in West Virginia

West Virginia's hospice and home care landscape is shaped by the state's mountainous geography, dispersed rural communities, and one of the oldest populations in the United States. West Virginia consistently has one of the highest rates of chronic disease in the country — including high rates of heart disease, COPD, cancer, and diabetes — which creates significant and sustained demand for hospice and palliative care services across all regions of the state.

Hospice Care Across West Virginia

Charleston, the state capital, is anchored by Charleston Area Medical Center (CAMC), the largest hospital system in the state, along with Thomas Memorial Hospital (now Thomas Health) and St. Francis Hospital (part of CAMC). CAMC operates a palliative care program and coordinates hospice transitions for complex medical patients across the Kanawha Valley.

Huntington is home to Cabell Huntington Hospital and St. Mary's Medical Center, both regional referral centers serving southwestern West Virginia and eastern Kentucky. Marshall University's Joan C. Edwards School of Medicine provides academic medicine resources to the region. The Huntington area has been significantly affected by the opioid crisis, and hospice providers in this region have developed expertise in complex pain and symptom management.

Morgantown is home to WVU Medicine (West Virginia University Health System), the state's academic medical center, with WVU Medicine Ruby Memorial Hospital serving as the primary Level I trauma center and academic referral hospital. WVU Medicine operates a palliative care program. The Eastern Panhandle (Martinsburg, Shepherdstown, Harpers Ferry) is geographically close to the Baltimore-Washington metro area and may have access to providers serving from Maryland and Virginia as well.

Medicare and West Virginia Medicaid Coverage

Medicare Part A covers hospice for eligible West Virginia patients with a physician-certified prognosis of six months or less. Covered services include nursing visits, comfort medications related to the terminal diagnosis, medical equipment (hospital bed, oxygen, wheelchair), home health aide visits, social work, chaplain services, and bereavement counseling — most at $0 to the patient or family.

West Virginia Medicaid covers hospice for qualifying low-income residents. Managed care plans include Aetna Better Health of West Virginia, The Health Plan, UniCare (Anthem), and WV Family Health. West Virginia also operates the Aged and Disabled Waiver (ADW) program, which provides home- and community-based services as an alternative to nursing facility care. Dual-eligible patients can coordinate Medicare and Medicaid benefits.

West Virginia licenses and regulates hospice providers through the West Virginia Department of Health and Human Resources (DHHR). All Medicare-certified West Virginia hospice providers meet state licensing requirements as a condition of federal certification.

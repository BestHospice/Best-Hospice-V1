Finding Hospice Care in South Carolina

South Carolina's hospice market spans three distinct regions: the Lowcountry coast anchored by Charleston and its rapidly growing suburbs, the Midlands centered on Columbia, and the Upstate anchored by Greenville and Spartanburg. The state's senior population has grown significantly as retirees from across the country have relocated to coastal communities, resort areas like Hilton Head and Myrtle Beach, and the milder climates of the Piedmont.

Hospice Care Across South Carolina's Regions

Charleston and the Lowcountry are served by MUSC Health (Medical University of South Carolina), one of the state's two academic medical centers, along with Roper St. Francis Healthcare and Trident Health (HCA). Mount Pleasant, North Charleston, and the surrounding communities have a large and growing senior population with strong hospice provider coverage. Confirming that a provider specifically covers your ZIP code is important in this geographically spread market.

Columbia and the Midlands are anchored by Prisma Health Midlands (formerly Palmetto Health) and the University of South Carolina School of Medicine. Lexington Medical Center and Richland County healthcare facilities also coordinate hospice referrals in this corridor.

Greenville and the Upstate are served by Prisma Health Upstate, formerly Greenville Health System, one of the largest hospital systems in the Carolinas. Bon Secours St. Francis Health System also operates in the Greenville area. The Upstate has strong hospital-based palliative care programs that transition patients to home or facility-based hospice.

Medicare and South Carolina Medicaid Coverage

Medicare Part A covers hospice for eligible South Carolina patients with a physician-certified prognosis of six months or less. Covered services include nursing visits, comfort medications related to the terminal diagnosis, medical equipment, home health aide visits, social work, chaplain services, and bereavement counseling — most at $0 to the patient or family.

SC Healthy Connections Medicaid covers hospice for qualifying low-income South Carolina residents. The SC Healthy Connections Prime program coordinates care for dual-eligible individuals (those with both Medicare and Medicaid). Medicare Advantage plans from BlueCross BlueShield of South Carolina, UnitedHealthcare, Aetna, and Humana are available statewide and must cover hospice at Medicare Part A levels.

South Carolina licenses and regulates hospice providers through the South Carolina Department of Health and Environmental Control (DHEC). All Medicare-certified South Carolina hospice providers meet state licensing requirements as a condition of federal certification.

South Carolina Hospice Laws, Licensing & Benefits

Certificate of Need (CON) — Important for South Carolina Families: South Carolina requires a Certificate of Need for hospice licensure. The number of licensed hospice providers per service area is regulated by the state — confirm that any provider you're considering holds an active South Carolina CON for your county before enrolling.

Advance Directive Form: South Carolina uses the South Carolina Advance Directive for Health Care to document end-of-life preferences and designate a healthcare agent. South Carolina also uses the POLST (Physician Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment) form as a portable medical order for seriously ill patients. Hospice teams can help families complete or update these documents when starting care.

Medicaid Home Care Programs: South Carolina Healthy Connections Medicaid offers the Choices in Long Term Care (Choices) waiver program, which provides home and community-based services for qualifying low-income seniors as an alternative to nursing facility placement. The Choices program is operated through the managed care plans (Absolute Total Care, Molina, Simply Healthcare, BlueChoice HealthPlan). Dual-eligible patients should confirm how their Healthy Connections managed care plan interacts with Medicare hospice benefits.

Hospice Licensing Authority: South Carolina licenses hospice providers through the South Carolina Department of Health and Environmental Control (DHEC), Division of Health Licensing. All Medicare-certified South Carolina hospice providers meet state licensing requirements as a condition of federal certification.

Free Medicare Counseling: South Carolina's SHIP program (administered through the SC Lieutenant Governor's Office on Aging) offers free Medicare counseling statewide. SHIP counselors can help families understand hospice benefits, CON-related provider availability, Healthy Connections Choices waiver coordination, and Medicare Advantage plans in Columbia, Charleston, Greenville, and across South Carolina.

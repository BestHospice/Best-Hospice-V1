Finding Hospice and Home Care in New York

New York has a large and well-established hospice and home care industry serving a diverse population across five boroughs of New York City, Long Island, Westchester, and upstate communities from Buffalo to Albany to Syracuse. The state's dense urban areas have significant concentrations of providers, while rural and suburban areas may have fewer options — making it important for families to identify which agencies serve their specific zip code.

Best Hospice and Home Health helps New York families compare verified providers by city, review coverage areas, and connect with local agencies directly. Use the search tool to find hospice and home care support near you.

Medicare and Medicaid in New York

Medicare covers hospice in New York for eligible beneficiaries with a physician-certified terminal diagnosis. New York Medicaid (Medicaid Managed Care and Medicaid Fee-for-Service) also covers hospice services for qualifying enrollees. New York's Managed Long Term Care (MLTC) program covers home-based personal care, aide services, and other long-term supports.

New York has significant Medicaid enrollment and a robust MLTC infrastructure. Families whose loved ones are enrolled in MLTC should confirm how the transition to hospice affects their plan, as hospice enrollment typically shifts billing responsibility from MLTC to the Medicare Hospice Benefit.

Hospice Care in New York: What Families Should Know

New York hospice providers are licensed and certified by the New York State Department of Health. Families can verify a provider's certification status through the DOH Health Profiles database. Medicare-certified hospices are also surveyed for compliance with federal Conditions of Participation.

New York's Health Care Proxy law allows individuals to designate a health care agent to make decisions on their behalf if they become unable to do so. If no proxy has been designated, New York law establishes a surrogate decision-making hierarchy. Families navigating these questions can ask the hospice social worker for guidance.

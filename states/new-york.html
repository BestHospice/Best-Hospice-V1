Finding Hospice Care in New York

New York has one of the largest and most established hospice industries in the country, shaped by a population of nearly 20 million people and a particularly well-developed infrastructure for end-of-life care in New York City and its suburbs. The state has a long history of strong healthcare regulation and consumer protection — New York hospice agencies are licensed and surveyed by the state Department of Health, and families have access to quality data through multiple public databases.

Hospice Care in New York City

New York City is one of the most competitive hospice markets in the United States, with dozens of licensed agencies serving all five boroughs. Major health systems that coordinate hospice transitions include NewYork-Presbyterian (Columbia University Irving Medical Center and Weill Cornell Medicine), NYU Langone Health, Mount Sinai Health System, Northwell Health (the largest healthcare provider in New York State), and NYC Health + Hospitals — the public safety-net system serving uninsured and Medicaid patients across the city.

New York City's extraordinary diversity — with large Spanish-speaking, Chinese, Russian, Haitian Creole, Korean, Bengali, and Arabic-speaking communities — means multilingual hospice services are widely available in the metro. Confirm language capabilities and specific borough or neighborhood coverage when inquiring.

Long Island (Nassau and Suffolk counties) and Westchester County have their own provider ecosystems, served by Northwell Health, NYU Langone, and a network of independent agencies. Confirm service area by ZIP code before beginning enrollment.

Hospice Care in Buffalo, Rochester, and Upstate New York

Buffalo and Western New York are served by Kaleida Health (Buffalo General Medical Center, Women & Children's Hospital of Buffalo), Catholic Health (Mercy Hospital, Sisters of Charity Hospital), and the University at Buffalo's academic medicine program. Roswell Park Comprehensive Cancer Center in Buffalo is a nationally recognized cancer center with a palliative care program that coordinates hospice transitions for oncology patients across Western New York.

Rochester is anchored by the University of Rochester Medical Center (Strong Memorial Hospital, Golisano Children's Hospital) and Rochester Regional Health. URMC's Palliative Care Program is one of the more established in Upstate New York and facilitates hospice referrals across the Finger Lakes region.

Albany and the Capital Region are served by Albany Medical Center (the only academic medical center in New York's 25-county Capital Region), St. Peter's Health Partners (Trinity Health), and Ellis Hospital (Nuvance Health). Syracuse is anchored by Upstate University Hospital and Crouse Health.

Medicare and New York Medicaid Coverage

Medicare Part A covers hospice for eligible New York patients with a physician-certified prognosis of six months or less. In 2026, covered services include nursing visits, comfort medications for the terminal diagnosis, medical equipment (hospital bed, oxygen, wheelchair), home health aide, social work, chaplain services, and bereavement counseling — most at $0 to the patient or family.

New York Medicaid covers hospice through both Managed Care and fee-for-service. The Managed Long Term Care (MLTC) program covers long-term home care — patients enrolled in MLTC transitioning to hospice should confirm how plan billing changes at enrollment. Medicaid Advantage Plus (MAP) plans also cover hospice. Medicare Advantage plans from UnitedHealthcare, Humana, Aetna, Emblem Health, and others are widely available — all must cover hospice at Medicare Part A levels.

New York licenses hospice providers through the NY State Department of Health. Families can verify a provider's certification and inspection history through the DOH Health Profiles database. New York's Health Care Proxy law allows individuals to designate a decision-making agent — hospice social workers can assist families in completing or updating these documents.

New York Hospice Laws, Licensing & Benefits

Certificate of Need (CON) — Important for New York Families: New York requires a Certificate of Need for hospice licensure through the New York State Department of Health. The number of licensed hospice providers per service area is regulated — confirm that any provider you're considering holds an active New York CON for your county or service area before enrolling.

Advance Directive Form: New York uses the MOLST (Medical Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment) form as the standard portable medical order for patients with serious illness. New York also uses a Health Care Proxy form to designate a healthcare agent, and a Living Will to document treatment preferences. The MOLST is honored by hospice teams, emergency responders, and hospitals statewide. Hospice teams can help families complete these documents.

Medicaid Home Care Programs: New York Medicaid offers the Managed Long Term Care (MLTC) program for qualifying seniors and people with disabilities who need home and community-based services. The Consumer Directed Personal Assistance Program (CDPAP) allows eligible recipients to hire and direct their own home care aides — including family members. Dual-eligible patients should confirm how their MLTC plan interacts with Medicare hospice benefits before enrolling.

Hospice Licensing Authority: New York licenses hospice providers through the New York State Department of Health, Division of Home and Community Based Care. All Medicare-certified New York hospice providers meet state licensing requirements as a condition of federal certification.

Free Medicare Counseling: New York's HIICAP (Health Insurance Information, Counseling and Assistance Program), administered by the New York State Office for the Aging, offers free Medicare counseling across all 62 counties. HIICAP counselors can help families understand hospice benefits, CON-related provider availability, MLTC/CDPAP interaction with hospice, and Medicare Advantage plans.

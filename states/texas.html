Finding Hospice Care in Texas

Texas is one of the largest hospice markets in the country, with more than 30 million residents and a rapidly growing senior population spread across enormous metropolitan areas, mid-size cities, and rural counties. The state's healthcare infrastructure ranges from the world-class academic and cancer systems of Houston and Dallas to community hospitals serving West Texas and the Panhandle, where access to specialized care requires more planning.

Hospice Care in Dallas–Fort Worth

The Dallas–Fort Worth Metroplex — covering Dallas, Tarrant, Collin, Denton, Rockwall, and surrounding counties — is one of the largest and fastest-growing hospice markets in the United States. The region's rapidly expanding senior population, driven by migration from across the country, creates consistent demand for hospice services across the entire Metroplex.

Major DFW health systems that coordinate hospice transitions include UT Southwestern Medical Center (one of the country's leading academic medical centers), Baylor Scott & White Health (the largest nonprofit health system in Texas), Texas Health Resources (Presbyterian, Harris Methodist, Arlington Memorial), Medical City Healthcare (HCA), and Methodist Health System. Parkland Memorial Hospital serves as the public safety-net hospital for Dallas County. Each system has palliative care and social work teams that facilitate hospice referrals.

Hospice Care in Houston

Houston is the fourth-largest city in the United States and home to the Texas Medical Center — the largest medical complex in the world. MD Anderson Cancer Center, Houston Methodist, Memorial Hermann, HCA Houston Healthcare, Baylor St. Luke's Medical Center, and Harris Health System (the public safety-net system) all coordinate hospice transitions for patients across Houston's vast geography.

Houston's diverse population — with large Hispanic, Vietnamese, Nigerian, and Chinese communities — means multilingual hospice services are frequently available in the metro area. When searching for a Houston hospice provider, confirm language capabilities and the specific suburbs they serve: The Woodlands, Sugar Land, Katy, Pearland, Pasadena, and Baytown each have their own provider landscapes.

Hospice Care in San Antonio and Austin

San Antonio is home to University Health (the public safety-net hospital and Level I trauma center), Baptist Health System, Methodist Healthcare, and CHRISTUS Health — a large Catholic health system with strong hospice and palliative care programs. San Antonio also has significant VA presence through the South Texas Veterans Health Care System, which serves veterans across South Texas.

Austin is anchored by UT Health Austin (the clinical practice of UT Dell Medical School), Ascension Seton, St. David's HealthCare, and Baylor Scott & White. Austin's rapid population growth has expanded demand for hospice services across Travis, Williamson, Hays, and Bastrop counties.

Medicare and Texas Medicaid Coverage

Medicare Part A covers hospice for eligible Texas patients with a physician-certified prognosis of six months or less. In 2026, covered services include nursing visits, comfort medications for the terminal diagnosis, medical equipment (hospital bed, oxygen, wheelchair), home health aide, social work, chaplain services, and bereavement counseling — most at $0 to the patient or family.

Texas Medicaid covers hospice through the STAR+PLUS managed care program for qualifying low-income residents with long-term care needs. STAR+PLUS managed care organizations include Amerigroup Texas, Molina Healthcare of Texas, Superior HealthPlan (Centene), and UnitedHealthcare Community Plan of Texas. Medicare Advantage plans from UnitedHealthcare, Humana, Aetna, and Blue Cross Blue Shield of Texas are widely available statewide — all must cover hospice at Medicare Part A levels.

Texas licenses and regulates hospice providers through the Texas Health and Human Services Commission. All Medicare-certified Texas hospice providers meet state licensing requirements as a condition of federal certification.

Texas Hospice Laws, Licensing & Benefits

Certificate of Need (CON): Texas does not require a Certificate of Need for hospice licensure. Any provider that meets state licensing and Medicare certification requirements may operate in Texas, giving families broad provider choices across Houston, Dallas, San Antonio, Austin, and statewide.

Advance Directive Form: Texas uses the Directive to Physicians, Family, or Surrogate (Texas living will) and the Medical Power of Attorney to document end-of-life preferences. Texas also uses the Out-of-Hospital Do-Not-Resuscitate (OOH DNR) Order, which functions similarly to a POLST as a portable medical order. Hospice teams can help families complete or update these Texas-specific documents when starting care.

Medicaid Home Care Programs: Texas Medicaid offers the STAR+PLUS HCBS Waiver, which provides home and community-based services (personal care, homemaker, respite care) for qualifying low-income adults aged 21 and older as an alternative to nursing facility placement. STAR+PLUS is administered through managed care organizations. The Community Living Assistance and Support Services (CLASS) program provides additional support for adults with related conditions. Dual-eligible patients should confirm how their STAR+PLUS plan interacts with Medicare hospice benefits.

Hospice Licensing Authority: Texas licenses hospice providers through the Texas Health and Human Services Commission (HHSC), Provider Finance Department. All Medicare-certified Texas hospice providers meet state licensing requirements as a condition of federal certification. Texas HHSC publishes inspection and survey data for licensed hospice providers.

Free Medicare Counseling: Texas's SHIP program (administered through the Texas Health and Human Services Commission) offers free Medicare counseling statewide. SHIP counselors can help families understand hospice benefits, STAR+PLUS waiver coordination, Texas advance directive requirements, and Medicare Advantage plans across Houston, Dallas, San Antonio, and Austin.

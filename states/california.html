Finding Hospice Care in California

California has one of the largest and most diverse hospice markets in the country, driven by a population of nearly 40 million people and a higher-than-average concentration of seniors in communities throughout the state — from Los Angeles and the Inland Empire to the San Francisco Bay Area, the Central Valley, and the San Diego–Tijuana corridor. The state has more licensed hospice agencies than any other in the United States, reflecting both its size and its strong cultural traditions around home-based, family-centered care.

Hospice Care in Los Angeles

Los Angeles County is one of the most competitive hospice markets in the country, with dozens of licensed agencies serving the county's 10 million residents. Major health systems coordinating hospice transitions include Cedars-Sinai Medical Center, UCLA Health (Ronald Reagan UCLA Medical Center and a large ambulatory network), USC Keck Medicine, Dignity Health (California Hospital Medical Center, Northridge Hospital), Providence Health (Providence Saint John's, Providence Holy Cross), and LAC+USC Medical Center — the public safety-net hospital for Los Angeles County.

Los Angeles's extraordinary ethnic and linguistic diversity — with large communities speaking Spanish, Mandarin, Cantonese, Korean, Armenian, Tagalog, and dozens of other languages — means multilingual hospice services are widely available in the metro. When searching for a provider, confirm language capabilities and the specific neighborhoods or ZIP codes they serve.

The Inland Empire (San Bernardino and Riverside counties), Orange County, and the San Fernando Valley each have their own provider landscapes. Confirm service area by ZIP code before beginning the enrollment process.

Hospice Care in San Diego

San Diego is a large hospice market served by UC San Diego Health (one of the leading academic medical centers in the West), Sharp HealthCare (Sharp Memorial, Sharp Grossmont, Sharp Chula Vista), Scripps Health (Scripps Mercy, Scripps La Jolla, Scripps Green), and Kaiser Permanente San Diego. The VA San Diego Healthcare System serves veterans and facilitates transitions to community hospice partners.

San Diego's proximity to the Mexican border and its large communities of Spanish-speaking, Filipino, and Vietnamese residents means multilingual providers are available throughout the county. North County communities (Carlsbad, Escondido, Vista) and East County (El Cajon, Santee) have their own provider networks — confirm service area coverage when inquiring.

Hospice Care in the Bay Area and Sacramento

The San Francisco Bay Area is anchored by UCSF Health (one of the top academic medical centers in the world), Stanford Health Care, Sutter Health (Alta Bates Summit, California Pacific Medical Center), John Muir Health, and Dignity Health. The Bay Area's concentration of tech industry workers and biomedical researchers has produced a more data-informed culture around healthcare decision-making — many Bay Area families research hospice options extensively before choosing a provider.

Sacramento and the Central Valley are served by UC Davis Health (the primary academic medical center for inland Northern California), Sutter Health Sacramento, and Dignity Health. The Central Valley's large agricultural communities and significant Latino population make Spanish-speaking hospice providers especially important in Fresno, Stockton, Modesto, and Bakersfield.

Medicare and Medi-Cal Coverage

Medicare Part A covers hospice for eligible California patients with a physician-certified prognosis of six months or less. In 2026, covered services include nursing visits, comfort medications for the terminal diagnosis, medical equipment (hospital bed, oxygen, wheelchair), home health aide, social work, chaplain services, and bereavement counseling — most at $0 to the patient or family.

Medi-Cal — California's Medicaid program — covers hospice for qualifying low-income residents. Medi-Cal managed care operates through county-based health plans: LA Care Health Plan in Los Angeles, Inland Empire Health Plan, Central California Alliance for Health, and Health Plan of San Mateo, among others. Dual-eligible patients may access coordinated care through the California Advancing and Innovating Medi-Cal (CalAIM) initiative. Medicare Advantage plans are widely available statewide — all must cover hospice at Medicare Part A levels.

California licenses and regulates hospice providers through the California Department of Public Health (CDPH). All Medicare-certified California hospice providers meet state licensing requirements as a condition of federal certification.

California Hospice Laws, Licensing & Benefits

Certificate of Need (CON): California does not require a Certificate of Need for hospice licensure. Any provider that meets state licensing and Medicare certification requirements may operate in California, giving families broad provider choices across the state's large and competitive hospice market.

Advance Directive Form: California uses the California Advance Health Care Directive (AHCD) to document healthcare preferences and designate a healthcare agent. California also uses the POLST (Physician Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment) form as a portable medical order for patients with serious illness. Hospice teams can help families complete or update these documents.

Medi-Cal Home and Community-Based Services: California's In-Home Supportive Services (IHSS) program provides Medi-Cal-funded personal care assistance to qualifying low-income seniors and people with disabilities — allowing them to remain at home safely. IHSS can complement hospice services for eligible patients. California Medi-Cal also covers hospice through managed care plans.

Hospice Licensing Authority: California licenses hospice providers through the California Department of Public Health (CDPH), Licensing and Certification Division. All Medicare-certified California hospice providers meet state licensing requirements as a condition of federal certification. California has some of the most detailed hospice quality reporting requirements in the country.

Free Medicare Counseling: California's HICAP (Health Insurance Counseling and Advocacy Program), administered by the California Department of Aging, provides free, unbiased Medicare counseling across all 58 counties. HICAP counselors can help families understand hospice benefits, Medi-Cal interaction with Medicare, and Medicare Advantage plans.

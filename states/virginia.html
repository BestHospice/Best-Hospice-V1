Finding Hospice Care in Virginia

Virginia's hospice landscape spans three very different regions: the dense Northern Virginia suburbs directly adjacent to Washington, D.C.; the Hampton Roads coastal metro anchored by Virginia Beach and Norfolk; and the inland corridor from Richmond southwest through Roanoke into the Blue Ridge and Appalachian foothills. Each region has its own hospital systems, Medicaid managed care networks, and provider concentrations that families need to understand when searching for care.

Hospice Care in Northern Virginia and the DC Suburbs

Northern Virginia — including Alexandria, Arlington, Fairfax County, Loudoun County, and Prince William County — is one of the most densely populated and highest-income regions in the country. Families in this corridor often have access to a wide range of hospice options, including providers with multilingual teams reflecting the region's large immigrant communities from Latin America, South Asia, and East Asia.

Major health systems coordinating hospice transitions in Northern Virginia include Inova Health System (Inova Fairfax Medical Campus, Inova Alexandria Hospital, Inova Mount Vernon Hospital), Kaiser Permanente Mid-Atlantic, and Virginia Hospital Center in Arlington. The density of physicians and palliative care specialists in this corridor means hospice referrals are typically faster than in rural parts of the state.

Hospice Care in Hampton Roads

Hampton Roads — covering Virginia Beach, Norfolk, Chesapeake, Newport News, Hampton, Portsmouth, and Suffolk — is the most populous metro area in Virginia. Sentara Healthcare is the dominant health system, with Sentara Norfolk General Hospital serving as the region's primary Level I trauma center and academic medical center in partnership with Eastern Virginia Medical School. Bon Secours Mercy Health also operates hospitals in the region.

Hampton Roads also has a large active duty and veteran population due to Naval Station Norfolk, the world's largest naval base, along with Langley Air Force Base and Fort Eustis. The Hampton VA Medical Center and the Hunter Holmes McGuire VA Medical Center in Richmond both operate veteran-specific palliative care and can coordinate hospice transitions for eligible veterans. If your loved one is a veteran, ask providers specifically about veteran-specialized hospice programs, which may include additional emotional, spiritual, and logistical support.

Virginia Beach and Chesapeake are geographically spread out, so confirming that a provider actively covers your specific zip code is important before enrollment.

Hospice Care in Richmond and Roanoke

Richmond is Virginia's capital and home to VCU Health (Virginia Commonwealth University Health System), one of Virginia's two academic medical centers, along with Bon Secours Richmond Health System and HCA Virginia Health System (Chippenham Hospital, Johnston-Willis Hospital). VCU Health has a dedicated palliative care program and coordinates hospice transitions for complex oncology and cardiovascular patients.

Roanoke and the New River Valley are served primarily by Carilion Clinic, a regional health system with its flagship at Carilion Roanoke Memorial Hospital. Carilion operates across southwest Virginia and maintains palliative care services. The region's more rural character means hospice providers covering Roanoke also typically extend coverage to smaller surrounding communities.

Medicare and Virginia Medicaid Coverage

Medicare Part A covers hospice for eligible Virginia patients with a physician-certified prognosis of six months or less. Covered services include nursing visits, comfort medications related to the terminal diagnosis, medical equipment (hospital bed, oxygen, wheelchair), home health aide visits, social work, chaplain services, and bereavement counseling — most at $0 to the patient or family.

Virginia Medicaid covers hospice through its Cardinal Care managed care program. Managed care organizations participating in Cardinal Care include Aetna Better Health of Virginia, Anthem HealthKeepers Plus, Molina Healthcare of Virginia, Optima Health Community Care, and UnitedHealthcare Community Plan of Virginia. Dual-eligible patients — those with both Medicare and Medicaid — may be able to coordinate both programs to minimize out-of-pocket costs. Medicare Advantage plans from Humana, UnitedHealthcare, and Anthem Blue Cross Blue Shield are widely available in Virginia and must cover hospice at Medicare Part A benefit levels.

Virginia licenses and regulates hospice providers through the Virginia Department of Health (VDH). All Medicare-certified Virginia hospice providers meet state licensing requirements as a condition of federal certification.

Virginia Hospice Laws, Licensing & Benefits

Certificate of Need (CON): Virginia does not require a Certificate of Need for hospice licensure. Any provider that meets state licensing and Medicare certification requirements may operate in Virginia, giving families broad provider choices across Northern Virginia, Hampton Roads, the Richmond metro, and communities statewide.

Advance Directive Form: Virginia uses the Virginia Advance Medical Directive to document end-of-life preferences and designate a healthcare agent. Virginia also uses the Virginia POLST (Physician Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment) form as a portable medical order for seriously ill patients. Hospice teams can help families complete or update these documents — the POLST must be signed by a physician, nurse practitioner, or physician assistant to be valid.

Medicaid Home Care Programs: Virginia Medicaid offers the Commonwealth Coordinated Care Plus (CCC Plus) Waiver, which provides home and community-based services for qualifying low-income seniors and adults with disabilities as an alternative to nursing facility placement. CCC Plus is operated through managed care organizations including Aetna Better Health, Anthem HealthKeepers Plus, Molina Healthcare, Optima Health, and UnitedHealthcare. Dual-eligible patients should confirm how their CCC Plus plan interacts with Medicare hospice benefits before enrolling.

Veteran Hospice Benefits: Virginia has a significant veteran population, particularly in Northern Virginia (near the Pentagon and Fort Belvoir), Hampton Roads (Naval Station Norfolk, Joint Base Langley-Eustis), and Richmond (McGuire VA Medical Center). Ask whether any hospice provider you're considering has a VA contract or veteran-specific program — Veterans Affairs (VA) hospice can be accessed through the VA system in addition to Medicare-funded community hospice.

Free Medicare Counseling: Virginia's VICAP (Virginia Insurance Counseling and Assistance Program), administered by the Virginia Department for Aging and Rehabilitative Services, offers free Medicare counseling statewide. VICAP counselors can help families understand hospice benefits, CCC Plus waiver coordination, and Medicare Advantage plans across Northern Virginia, Hampton Roads, and the Richmond metro.

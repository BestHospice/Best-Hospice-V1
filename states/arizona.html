Palliative Care vs. Hospice Care in Arizona

Many Arizona families encounter both terms — palliative care and hospice care — when navigating a serious diagnosis. The key difference: palliative care can begin at any stage of illness and run alongside curative treatment, while hospice care is specifically for patients with a terminal prognosis of six months or less who have decided to focus on comfort rather than cure.

If your loved one is still undergoing treatment but struggling with pain, fatigue, or other symptoms, palliative care may be the right starting point. If treatment is no longer working or no longer wanted, and the goal has shifted to the best possible quality of life during the time remaining, hospice care is likely the right choice.

Medicare and AHCCCS Coverage in Arizona

Medicare Part A covers hospice services for eligible Arizona patients when a physician certifies a terminal prognosis of six months or less. Covered services typically include nursing visits, comfort medications tied to the terminal diagnosis, medical equipment such as hospital beds and oxygen, home health aide support, social work, chaplain services, and bereavement counseling for the family.

Arizona's Medicaid program — AHCCCS (Arizona Health Care Cost Containment System) — also covers hospice for qualifying low-income patients and can coordinate with Medicare for dual-eligible patients. Out-of-pocket costs for dual-eligible patients are typically minimal to none. Private insurance coverage varies by plan.

How to Choose the Best Hospice Care in Arizona

When comparing hospice providers in Phoenix, Tucson, and across Arizona, ask each agency how quickly they can begin intake, whether nursing support is available after hours and on weekends, and how they communicate with family members. Strong providers respond fast, set clear expectations, and coordinate directly with your loved one's physician.

Common qualifying diagnoses for hospice in Arizona include advanced cancer, end-stage heart or lung disease, late-stage dementia and Alzheimer's, kidney failure, ALS, and advanced Parkinson's disease. Arizona Hospice Laws, Licensing & Benefits

Certificate of Need (CON): Arizona does not require a Certificate of Need for hospice licensure. Any provider that meets state licensing and Medicare certification standards may operate in Arizona, which means families generally have more provider choices than in CON-regulated states.

Advance Directive Form: Arizona uses the Arizona Health Care Directive (a living will and healthcare power of attorney) along with the POLST (Physician Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment) form as a portable medical order for seriously ill patients. Hospice teams can help families complete or update these documents when starting care.

ALTCS and HCBS Programs: Arizona's Long Term Care System (ALTCS) is the state's Medicaid program for long-term services, including in-home care and hospice support for qualifying low-income seniors and people with disabilities. ALTCS is administered through AHCCCS. Arizona is one of the few states where ALTCS can supplement Medicare hospice benefits for dual-eligible patients.

Hospice Licensing Authority: Arizona licenses hospice providers through the Arizona Department of Health Services (ADHS). All Medicare-certified Arizona hospice providers meet state licensing requirements as a condition of federal certification.

Free Medicare Counseling: Arizona's SHIP program (administered through the Arizona Department of Economic Security's Aging and Adult Services) offers free, unbiased Medicare counseling to help families understand hospice benefits, ALTCS interaction with Medicare, and Medicare Advantage plans.

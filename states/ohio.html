Finding Hospice Care in Ohio

Ohio has a strong tradition of community-based hospice care, with well-established providers serving major metros including Columbus, Cleveland, Cincinnati, Dayton, and Akron, as well as smaller communities throughout the state. Ohio is consistently among the top states nationally for hospice utilization, with a high percentage of Medicare beneficiaries receiving hospice care at end of life — a reflection of the state's strong provider infrastructure and community awareness.

Hospice Care Across Ohio's Major Cities

Columbus is home to The Ohio State University Wexner Medical Center, one of the country's leading academic medical centers and cancer research institutions. OhioHealth (Riverside Methodist Hospital, Grant Medical Center) and Mount Carmel Health System are the other major Columbus-area health systems, each with palliative care programs coordinating hospice transitions.

Cleveland is anchored by Cleveland Clinic, consistently ranked among the top hospitals in the United States, with particular strength in cardiac and oncology care. University Hospitals Cleveland Medical Center and MetroHealth Medical Center (the public safety-net hospital) also serve the greater Cleveland area. The density of academic medicine in Cleveland and surrounding Summit, Lorain, and Lake counties means hospice referral pathways are well-established.

Cincinnati is served by UC Health (University of Cincinnati Medical Center), Cincinnati Children's Hospital Medical Center, TriHealth (Good Samaritan Hospital, Bethesda North Hospital), and Mercy Health (St. Elizabeth, Fairfield Hospital). The Cincinnati metro extends into northern Kentucky and southeastern Indiana, and many providers serving this tri-state region operate across county and state lines.

Medicare and Ohio Medicaid Coverage

Medicare covers hospice in Ohio for eligible beneficiaries with a physician-certified terminal diagnosis and a prognosis of six months or less. Ohio Medicaid also covers hospice services for qualifying enrollees. MyCare Ohio coordinates care for dual-eligible individuals (those with both Medicare and Medicaid) through managed care plans including Aetna Better Health of Ohio, Buckeye Health Plan (Centene), CareSource, Molina Healthcare of Ohio, and UnitedHealthcare Community Plan of Ohio. Ohio's PASSPORT program provides home- and community-based services for older adults and individuals with disabilities as an alternative to nursing facility care.

Ohio Medicaid enrollees in a managed care plan should confirm with the hospice provider how enrollment affects their current coverage before signing hospice election paperwork.

Ohio law allows individuals to designate a healthcare power of attorney to make medical decisions when they are unable to do so. Living wills and DNR orders are also valid in Ohio. The hospice team's social worker can help families navigate advance directive documents and ensure the care plan reflects the patient's wishes.

Ohio Hospice Laws, Licensing & Benefits

Certificate of Need (CON): Ohio does not require a Certificate of Need for hospice licensure. Any provider that meets state licensing and Medicare certification requirements may operate in Ohio, giving families broad provider choices across Columbus, Cleveland, Cincinnati, and statewide.

Advance Directive Form: Ohio uses the MOLST (Medical Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment) form as its portable medical order for patients with serious illness. Ohio also uses a Durable Power of Attorney for Health Care and a Declaration (living will). The MOLST travels with the patient and is honored by hospice teams, emergency responders, and hospitals. Hospice teams can help families complete or update these documents when starting care.

Medicaid Home Care Programs: Ohio Medicaid offers the PASSPORT HCBS Waiver, which provides home and community-based services (personal care, homemaker, respite care, adult day services) for qualifying low-income seniors as an alternative to nursing facility placement. MyCare Ohio, the dual-eligible integrated care program, coordinates Medicare and Medicaid benefits in several counties. Ask your hospice provider whether PASSPORT services can continue alongside or complement hospice care for dual-eligible patients.

Hospice Licensing Authority: Ohio licenses hospice providers through the Ohio Department of Health, Bureau of Survey and Certification. All Medicare-certified Ohio hospice providers meet state licensing requirements as a condition of federal certification.

Free Medicare Counseling: Ohio's OSHIIP (Ohio Senior Health Insurance Information Program), administered by the Ohio Department of Insurance, offers free Medicare counseling across the state. OSHIIP counselors can help families understand hospice benefits, PASSPORT waiver interaction with hospice, MyCare Ohio dual-eligible coordination, and Medicare Advantage plans in Columbus, Cleveland, and Cincinnati.

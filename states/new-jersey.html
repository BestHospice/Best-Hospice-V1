Finding Hospice Care in New Jersey

New Jersey's hospice market is shaped by the state's position between two major metropolitan centers — New York City to the north and Philadelphia to the southwest — creating a dense network of healthcare providers and hospice agencies serving communities across all 21 counties. Northern New Jersey communities in Essex, Bergen, Union, and Hudson counties are tightly connected to New York City's academic medical systems. Southern New Jersey communities in Camden, Burlington, and Gloucester counties have strong ties to Philadelphia's major health systems.

Hospice Care in Northern and Southern New Jersey

Northern New Jersey is served by major health systems including RWJBarnabas Health (the largest integrated health system in New Jersey), Hackensack Meridian Health, Atlantic Health System (Morristown Medical Center, Overlook Medical Center), and Valley Health System. Each has palliative care programs and social work teams that coordinate hospice referrals. Newark and Jersey City are served by systems including Newark Beth Israel Medical Center and Jersey City Medical Center (RWJBarnabas).

Southern New Jersey is anchored by Cooper University Health Care in Camden and the Jefferson Health network, which serves communities across South Jersey. The Mount Laurel and Burlington County corridor has a significant senior population and active hospice provider market. Trenton and Mercer County are served by Capital Health System.

Medicare and New Jersey Medicaid Coverage

Medicare Part A covers hospice for eligible New Jersey patients with a physician-certified prognosis of six months or less. Covered services include nursing visits, comfort medications, medical equipment, home health aide visits, social work, chaplain services, and bereavement counseling — most at $0 to the patient or family.

New Jersey Medicaid (NJ FamilyCare) covers hospice for qualifying low-income residents. Participating managed care plans include Aetna Better Health of New Jersey, Horizon NJ Health, UnitedHealthcare Community Plan of New Jersey, and WellCare of New Jersey. Dual-eligible patients can coordinate Medicare and NJ FamilyCare benefits. Medicare Advantage plans from Aetna, Humana, UnitedHealthcare, and Horizon Blue Cross Blue Shield of New Jersey are widely available and must cover hospice at Medicare Part A levels.

New Jersey licenses and regulates hospice providers through the New Jersey Department of Health. All Medicare-certified New Jersey hospice providers meet state licensing requirements as a condition of federal certification.

Alzheimer's & Dementia Hospice Care in New Jersey

Dementia — including Alzheimer's disease — is one of the most common diagnoses in hospice care nationwide, and New Jersey's large senior population means this need is especially significant. New Jersey families caring for a loved one with late-stage dementia often ask when hospice becomes appropriate and how to find providers with genuine dementia expertise.

A dementia patient typically qualifies for hospice in New Jersey when they reach late-stage disease: unable to walk independently, unable to communicate more than a few words, fully dependent on others for all daily care, and when a physician certifies a life expectancy of six months or less. These criteria align with Stage 7 on the Functional Assessment Staging Tool (FAST).

What hospice adds for dementia patients that standard care does not:

  • Non-verbal pain assessment — hospice-trained nurses recognize distress through body language and behavior when the patient cannot speak
  • Medications to manage agitation, anxiety, and pain without requiring verbal communication from the patient
  • Skin care and pressure wound prevention for bedbound patients
  • Safe swallowing guidance and feeding support as dysphagia progresses
  • 24/7 on-call nurse access — critical for families managing late-night crises at home
  • Caregiver training and emotional support for family members experiencing anticipatory grief

There is no time limit on Medicare hospice coverage for dementia patients in New Jersey. As long as a physician recertifies eligibility at each benefit period, coverage continues — some dementia patients remain on hospice for more than a year.

When evaluating NJ hospice providers for a dementia patient, ask specifically: "How do you assess pain in patients who cannot communicate verbally?" and "How many of your current patients have a dementia diagnosis?" A strong dementia hospice program will answer both questions with clarity and confidence. See our full guide: Hospice Care for Dementia & Alzheimer's.

New Jersey Hospice Laws, Licensing & Benefits

Certificate of Need (CON): New Jersey does not require a Certificate of Need for hospice licensure. Any provider that meets state licensing and Medicare certification requirements may operate in New Jersey, giving families broad provider choices across North, Central, and South Jersey.

Advance Directive Form: New Jersey uses the New Jersey Advance Directive for Health Care (combining a living will and proxy directive) to document end-of-life preferences and designate a healthcare representative. New Jersey also uses the POLST (Physician Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment) form as a portable medical order for seriously ill patients. Hospice teams can help families complete or update these documents.

Medicaid Home Care Programs: New Jersey FamilyCare (Medicaid) offers the Personal Preference Program (PPP), which allows qualifying recipients to self-direct home care services, and Managed Long Term Services and Supports (MLTSS) for qualifying seniors and people with disabilities. PACE (Program of All-inclusive Care for the Elderly) sites also operate in New Jersey. Dual-eligible patients should confirm how NJ FamilyCare managed care plans interact with Medicare hospice benefits.

Hospice Licensing Authority: New Jersey licenses hospice providers through the New Jersey Department of Health, Division of Health Facilities Evaluation and Licensing. All Medicare-certified New Jersey hospice providers meet state licensing requirements as a condition of federal certification.

Free Medicare Counseling: New Jersey's SHIP program (administered through the New Jersey Division of Aging Services) offers free, unbiased Medicare counseling statewide. SHIP counselors can help families understand hospice benefits, NJ FamilyCare MLTSS coordination, and Medicare Advantage plans across the state's North, Central, and South Jersey markets.

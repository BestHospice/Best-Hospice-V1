Finding Hospice Care in Florida

Florida has one of the largest senior populations in the United States and one of the most active hospice markets in the country. With more than 22 million residents and a higher-than-average percentage of adults over 65 — particularly in Southwest Florida, the Tampa Bay area, and the Space Coast — demand for quality hospice care is high across the state.

Hospice Care in Tampa Bay

The Tampa Bay area — covering Hillsborough, Pinellas, Pasco, and Hernando counties — is one of Florida's most active hospice markets. The region's large retiree population, including the massive Sun City Center retirement community in southern Hillsborough County, creates consistent demand for hospice services.

Major Tampa Bay health systems coordinating hospice transitions include Tampa General Hospital (the region's Level I trauma center and USF Health affiliate), BayCare Health System (St. Joseph's, Morton Plant, Mease), HCA Florida (Brandon, Trinity, Largo), AdventHealth Tampa, and Moffitt Cancer Center — the state's only NCI-designated Comprehensive Cancer Center, with an active palliative care program.

Hospice Care in Miami, Orlando, and Jacksonville

Miami and South Florida are served by Jackson Health System (the public safety-net hospital for Miami-Dade), Baptist Health South Florida, and a large network of independent hospice agencies. Miami's diverse population — with large Cuban, Haitian, Venezuelan, and Colombian communities — means Spanish-speaking and multilingual hospice services are widely available.

Orlando is anchored by AdventHealth (one of the largest nonprofit hospital systems in the country), Orlando Health (Orlando Regional Medical Center, Arnold Palmer Hospital), and HCA Florida. The region serves Orange, Seminole, Osceola, and Lake counties, with significant retirement populations in The Villages (Sumter County) nearby.

Jacksonville and Northeast Florida are served by Mayo Clinic Jacksonville, UF Health Jacksonville, Baptist Health, and Ascension St. Vincent's. Jacksonville's large geographic footprint means confirming your specific ZIP code's coverage area is especially important before enrolling with a provider.

Medicare and Florida Medicaid Coverage

Medicare Part A covers hospice for eligible Florida patients with a physician-certified prognosis of six months or less. In 2026, covered services include nursing visits, comfort medications for the terminal diagnosis, medical equipment (hospital bed, oxygen, wheelchair), home health aide, social work, chaplain services, and bereavement counseling — most at $0 to the patient or family.

Florida Medicaid covers hospice for qualifying low-income residents through the Statewide Medicaid Managed Care (SMMC) program. Plans include Humana Florida, Molina Healthcare of Florida, Simply Healthcare, Sunshine Health (Centene), and UnitedHealthcare of Florida. Medicare Advantage plans are widely used in Florida — all must cover hospice at Medicare Part A levels. Verify your specific plan with the provider.

Florida licenses and regulates hospice providers through the Agency for Health Care Administration (AHCA). All Medicare-certified Florida hospice providers meet state licensing requirements as a condition of federal certification.

Florida Hospice Laws, Licensing & Benefits

Certificate of Need (CON) — Important for Florida Families: Florida is one of the few remaining states that requires a Certificate of Need for hospice licensure. This means the number of licensed hospice providers per service area is strictly limited by state regulation — not every agency is licensed to serve every ZIP code. Before enrolling with any Florida hospice provider, confirm that they hold an active CON for your specific service area. Families in some communities may have fewer licensed choices than in non-CON states.

Advance Directive Form: Florida uses the Florida Advance Directive (a living will and healthcare surrogate designation) to document end-of-life care preferences. Florida also uses the POLST (Physician Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment) form as a portable medical order for seriously ill patients. Hospice teams can help families complete or update these documents when starting care.

Florida Medicaid Long-Term Care: Florida's Statewide Medicaid Managed Care Long-Term Care (SMMC-LTC) program provides home and community-based services to qualifying low-income seniors and people with disabilities. SMMC-LTC plans can fund home care services that complement hospice, and dual-eligible patients can coordinate both programs. PACE (Program of All-inclusive Care for the Elderly) sites operate in several Florida metros.

Hospice Licensing Authority: Florida licenses and regulates hospice providers through the Agency for Health Care Administration (AHCA). AHCA publishes inspection reports and quality data for all licensed Florida hospice agencies — families can review these before choosing a provider.

Free Medicare Counseling: Florida's SHINE (Serving Health Insurance Needs of Elders) program offers free, unbiased Medicare counseling statewide. SHINE counselors can help families understand hospice benefits, CON-related provider limitations, Medicare Advantage hospice coverage, and Florida Medicaid coordination. Contact the Florida Department of Elder Affairs or call 1-800-96-ELDER to reach a SHINE counselor.

Finding Hospice and Home Care in Illinois

Illinois has a large and diverse hospice and home care market. The Chicago metro area — including Cook, DuPage, Lake, Kane, Will, and McHenry counties — has the highest concentration of providers, but quality options also exist across central and southern Illinois cities such as Peoria, Rockford, Springfield, and Champaign. Illinois families benefit from understanding which specific agencies serve their area, as coverage zones can vary significantly.

Medicare and Medicaid in Illinois

Medicare covers hospice in Illinois for eligible beneficiaries with a physician-certified terminal diagnosis. Illinois Medicaid (Illinois Medical Assistance Program) also covers hospice services for qualifying enrollees. The Illinois Department on Aging's Community Care Program provides home-based services — including personal care and homemaker services — to eligible older adults as an alternative or complement to institutional care.

Illinois has significant Medicaid managed care enrollment through HealthChoice Illinois. Families should confirm with the hospice provider how enrollment affects current Medicaid benefits. Hospice typically becomes the primary payer for hospice-related services, with Medicaid covering supplemental costs.

Hospice Care in Illinois: What Families Should Know

Illinois hospice agencies are licensed by the Illinois Department of Public Health. Medicare-certified hospices are subject to both state licensing requirements and federal Conditions of Participation. Families can review inspection history through the IDPH website and quality data through Medicare's Care Compare tool.

Illinois law recognizes both healthcare powers of attorney and living wills. Individuals can also complete a Physician Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment (POLST) form, which travels with the patient and communicates their wishes to all care providers including emergency responders. The hospice social worker can help ensure advance directives are in order.

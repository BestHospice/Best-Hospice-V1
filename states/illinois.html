Finding Hospice Care in Illinois

Illinois has a large and diverse hospice market. The Chicago metro area — including Cook, DuPage, Lake, Kane, Will, and McHenry counties — has the highest concentration of providers, but quality options also exist across central and southern Illinois in Peoria, Rockford, Springfield, Champaign, and smaller communities. Illinois families benefit from understanding which specific agencies serve their ZIP code, as service territories can vary significantly between providers operating in the same metropolitan area.

BestHospice.com helps Illinois families compare verified providers by city, review coverage areas, and connect with local agencies without referral fees or sales pressure.

Hospice Care in the Chicago Metro Area

Chicago's academic and hospital infrastructure is among the most comprehensive in the country. Northwestern Medicine (Northwestern Memorial Hospital), University of Chicago Medicine (UChicago Medicine), Rush University Medical Center, Loyola University Medical Center (Trinity Health), and the University of Illinois Hospital are the primary academic referral centers for complex cases. Each has dedicated palliative care programs that coordinate hospice transitions. Advocate Health (now Advocate Aurora Health) operates the largest private hospital network in Illinois with hospitals across the metro area including Advocate Christ Medical Center in Oak Lawn and Advocate Sherman Hospital in Elgin.

Chicago's diverse neighborhoods — with large Latino, Polish, Chinese, Korean, Filipino, and African American communities — mean multilingual hospice services are frequently available in the metro area. When searching for a Chicago-area provider, confirm they serve your specific suburb, as provider coverage zones vary widely across Cook, DuPage, Kane, Lake, McHenry, and Will counties.

Medicare and Illinois Medicaid Coverage

Medicare covers hospice in Illinois for eligible beneficiaries with a physician-certified terminal diagnosis. Illinois Medicaid (Illinois Medical Assistance Program) also covers hospice services for qualifying enrollees through the HealthChoice Illinois managed care program. Plans include Aetna Better Health of Illinois, Blue Cross Community Health Plans, CountyCare (Cook County Health), Harmony Health Plan, IlliniCare Health (Centene), Meridian Health Plan of Illinois, Molina Healthcare of Illinois, and NextLevel Health Partners.

The Illinois Department on Aging's Community Care Program (CCP) provides home-based services — including personal care and homemaker services — to eligible older adults through the Community Care Program as an alternative or complement to institutional care. Families with loved ones enrolled in both Medicare and Medicaid should confirm with the hospice provider how managed care enrollment affects current Medicaid benefits.

Illinois law recognizes healthcare powers of attorney and living wills. Individuals can also complete a Physician Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment (POLST) form to ensure their wishes are communicated to all care providers. The hospice social worker can help ensure advance directives are in order before care begins.

Illinois Hospice Laws, Licensing & Benefits

Certificate of Need (CON): Illinois does not require a Certificate of Need for hospice licensure. Any provider that meets state licensing and Medicare certification requirements may operate in Illinois, giving families broad provider choices across Chicago and statewide.

Advance Directive Form: Illinois uses the Illinois Statutory Short Form Power of Attorney for Health Care and the Declaration (living will) to document end-of-life preferences. Illinois also uses the POLST (Physician Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment) form as a portable medical order for seriously ill patients. Hospice teams can help families complete or update these documents when starting care.

Medicaid Home Care Programs: Illinois Medicaid (HealthChoice Illinois) funds the Home Services Program (HSP), which provides personal care and homemaker services for qualifying low-income adults with disabilities as an alternative to institutional care. PACE (Program of All-inclusive Care for the Elderly) sites operate in Chicago and suburban Cook County. Dual-eligible patients should confirm how HealthChoice Illinois plans interact with Medicare hospice benefits.

Hospice Licensing Authority: Illinois licenses hospice providers through the Illinois Department of Public Health (IDPH). All Medicare-certified Illinois hospice providers meet state licensing requirements as a condition of federal certification.

Free Medicare Counseling: Illinois's SHIP program (administered through the Illinois Department on Aging) offers free Medicare counseling statewide. SHIP counselors can help families understand hospice benefits, HealthChoice Illinois managed care plans, and Medicare Advantage coverage across the Chicago metro and downstate communities.

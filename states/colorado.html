Finding Hospice Care in Colorado

Colorado has a well-developed hospice market anchored by the Denver metro's large academic medical systems and extending across the Front Range to Fort Collins, Boulder, Colorado Springs, and Pueblo — as well as mountain communities and the Western Slope. The state's growing senior population, combined with a strong cultural emphasis on quality of life and patient autonomy, makes Colorado one of the more hospice-engaged states in the country.

Hospice Care in Denver

The Denver metro — covering Denver, Jefferson, Arapahoe, Adams, Douglas, and Broomfield counties — is the primary hub of Colorado's healthcare infrastructure. Major health systems coordinating hospice transitions include UCHealth (University of Colorado Hospital, one of the leading academic medical centers in the West), SCL Health (now Intermountain Health, operating St. Joseph Hospital, Good Samaritan Medical Center, and Lutheran Medical Center), HealthONE (HCA Healthcare, operating Rose Medical Center, Sky Ridge Medical Center, and Swedish Medical Center), and Centura Health (now CommonSpirit Health Colorado, operating St. Anthony Hospital and a network of facilities).

The University of Colorado School of Medicine's palliative care program at UCHealth is one of the more robust in the Mountain West, and many Denver hospice providers have strong referral relationships with the academic medical center. Denver Health, the public safety-net hospital for Denver County, also facilitates hospice transitions for uninsured and Medicaid-covered patients.

Hospice Care in Fort Collins, Boulder, and the Northern Front Range

The Northern Front Range — Fort Collins, Loveland, Greeley, and Boulder — is served by UCHealth Poudre Valley Hospital (Fort Collins), Banner Health (McKee Medical Center in Loveland, North Colorado Medical Center in Greeley), and Boulder Community Health. UCHealth's northern network has palliative care integration at Poudre Valley, which is the primary referral center for Larimer County.

Boulder County's mix of younger residents and a significant retiree community creates a distinctive hospice market. Providers serving Boulder often emphasize home-based care and open communication about end-of-life options. Families in Longmont, Lafayette, and Louisville should confirm which providers serve their specific ZIP code, as service areas can vary.

Hospice Care in Colorado Springs and Southern Colorado

Colorado Springs is anchored by UCHealth Memorial Hospital (the primary trauma and tertiary care center for El Paso County) and CommonSpirit Health's Penrose-St. Francis Health Services. Colorado Springs has a large military-connected population through Fort Carson and the Air Force Academy — the UCHealth VA clinic and the VA Eastern Colorado Health Care System serve veterans transitioning to hospice care in the region.

Pueblo and the Arkansas Valley are primarily served by Parkview Medical Center and St. Mary-Corwin Medical Center (CommonSpirit). Grand Junction and the Western Slope are served by St. Mary's Medical Center (CommonSpirit) and Community Hospital. Families in mountain communities — including Aspen, Vail, Steamboat Springs, and Durango — should confirm availability with providers, as rural coverage areas require more advance planning.

Medicare and Colorado Medicaid Coverage

Medicare Part A covers hospice for eligible Colorado patients with a physician-certified prognosis of six months or less. In 2026, covered services include nursing visits, comfort medications for the terminal diagnosis, medical equipment (hospital bed, oxygen, wheelchair), home health aide, social work, chaplain services, and bereavement counseling — most at $0 to the patient or family.

Health First Colorado — the state's Medicaid program — covers hospice for qualifying low-income residents. Colorado uses Regional Accountable Entities (RAEs) to coordinate physical health managed care across seven regions. Medicare Advantage plans from UnitedHealthcare, Humana, Aetna, and Cigna are widely available along the Front Range — all must cover hospice at Medicare Part A levels.

Colorado licenses and regulates hospice providers through the Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment (CDPHE). All Medicare-certified Colorado hospice providers meet state licensing requirements as a condition of federal certification.

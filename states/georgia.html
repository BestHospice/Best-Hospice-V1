Finding Hospice Care in Georgia

Georgia is home to more than 11 million residents and one of the fastest-growing senior populations in the Southeast. The state's healthcare infrastructure ranges from the large academic and specialty systems of Atlanta to community hospitals in Savannah, Augusta, Macon, and Columbus, extending to rural counties across south Georgia where access to care can require more planning.

Hospice Care in Atlanta

The Atlanta metropolitan area — covering Fulton, DeKalb, Gwinnett, Cobb, Clayton, Cherokee, Forsyth, and surrounding counties — is Georgia's largest hospice market. The region has a large and growing senior population spread across established intown neighborhoods and rapidly expanding suburbs north, south, east, and west of the city.

Major Atlanta health systems that coordinate hospice transitions include Emory Healthcare (Emory University Hospital, Emory Saint Joseph's), Piedmont Healthcare (Piedmont Atlanta, Piedmont Henry, Piedmont Newnan), WellStar Health System (Kennestone, Marietta, Cobb), Northside Hospital (Atlanta and Cherokee campuses), and Grady Health System (the public safety-net hospital serving underserved Atlanta residents). Each has case management and palliative care teams that facilitate hospice referrals.

When choosing an Atlanta hospice provider, confirm the coverage area for your specific suburb or neighborhood. Providers based in Marietta may not routinely serve Decatur or Stone Mountain, and agencies focused on Gwinnett County may have longer response times in Fayette or Henry counties.

Hospice Care in Savannah, Augusta, and Beyond

Savannah and coastal Georgia attract retirees drawn to the city's historic character and mild climate. St. Joseph's/Candler Health System and Memorial Health University Medical Center are the primary Savannah hospitals coordinating hospice transitions. Savannah hospice providers typically serve Chatham, Bryan, Effingham, and Liberty counties.

Augusta is home to Augusta University Medical Center (the primary academic medical center for east Georgia and western South Carolina), Piedmont Augusta (formerly University Hospital), and the Charlie Norwood VA Medical Center — which serves veterans across the Central Savannah River Area through VA and MISSION Act community care hospice programs.

Macon and Columbus are anchored by Atrium Health Navicent and Piedmont Columbus Regional respectively. Both have palliative care and case management teams for hospice transitions in middle and western Georgia.

Medicare and Georgia Medicaid Coverage

Medicare Part A covers hospice for eligible Georgia patients with a physician-certified prognosis of six months or less. In 2026, covered services include nursing visits, comfort medications for the terminal diagnosis, medical equipment (hospital bed, oxygen, wheelchair), home health aide, social work, chaplain services, and bereavement counseling — most at $0 to the patient or family.

Georgia Medicaid covers hospice for qualifying low-income residents through managed care organizations including Amerigroup Georgia, CareSource Georgia, Peach State Health Management (Centene), and WellCare of Georgia. Medicare Advantage plans from UnitedHealthcare, Humana, Aetna, and Blue Cross Blue Shield of Georgia are widely available statewide — all must cover hospice at Medicare Part A levels. Verify your specific plan with the provider.

Georgia licenses and regulates hospice providers through the Georgia Department of Community Health's Healthcare Facility Regulation division. All Medicare-certified Georgia hospice providers meet state licensing requirements as a condition of federal certification.

Georgia Hospice Laws, Licensing & Benefits

Certificate of Need (CON) — Important for Georgia Families: Georgia requires a Certificate of Need for hospice licensure. The number of licensed hospice providers per service area is regulated by the state — confirm that any provider you're considering holds an active Georgia CON for your specific county or ZIP code before enrolling.

Advance Directive Form: Georgia uses the Georgia Advance Directive for Health Care to document end-of-life wishes and designate a healthcare agent. Georgia also uses the POLST (Physician Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment) form as a portable medical order. Hospice teams can help families complete these documents when starting care.

Medicaid Home Care Programs: Georgia Medicaid offers the Community Care Services Program (CCSP) and the SOURCE (Service Options Using Resources in a Community Environment) waiver to provide home and community-based services for qualifying low-income seniors. These programs can fund home care services that complement hospice for dual-eligible patients. Contact the Georgia Department of Community Health for eligibility information.

Hospice Licensing Authority: Georgia licenses hospice providers through the Georgia Department of Community Health, Healthcare Facility Regulation Division. All Medicare-certified Georgia hospice providers meet state licensing requirements as a condition of federal certification.

Free Medicare Counseling: Georgia's GeorgiaCares program (the state SHIP) offers free, unbiased Medicare counseling to help families understand hospice benefits, CON provider limitations, Medicare Advantage plans, and Georgia Medicaid coordination. Contact the Georgia Division of Aging Services to reach a GeorgiaCares counselor.

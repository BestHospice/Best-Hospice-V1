Finding Hospice Care in Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania has one of the largest senior populations in the United States, and its hospice sector reflects that reality — with strong provider concentrations in Philadelphia, Pittsburgh, and their suburbs, alongside well-developed community hospice programs in mid-size cities like Allentown, Scranton, Erie, and Reading. The Philadelphia suburbs in Chester County and Montgomery County — including communities like Coatesville, Malvern, and Royersford — have strong provider networks given their proximity to several major academic health systems.

Hospice Care in Philadelphia and Pittsburgh

Philadelphia is home to one of the densest concentrations of academic medical centers in the country: Penn Medicine (Hospital of the University of Pennsylvania, Penn Presbyterian), Jefferson Health (Thomas Jefferson University Hospital), Temple Health, Drexel Medicine, and Children's Hospital of Philadelphia. Each system has palliative care teams coordinating hospice transitions, particularly for complex oncology, cardiac, and pulmonary patients. The Philadelphia suburbs in Chester and Montgomery counties have a large retirement community population and high hospice utilization.

Pittsburgh is anchored by UPMC (the University of Pittsburgh Medical Center), one of the largest integrated health systems in the country, along with Allegheny Health Network (AHN). UPMC operates extensive palliative care and hospice programs across western Pennsylvania. The North Huntingdon and Westmoreland County area is within UPMC's broad service region.

Medicare and Pennsylvania Medicaid Coverage

Medicare Part A covers hospice for eligible Pennsylvania patients with a physician-certified prognosis of six months or less. Covered services include nursing visits, comfort medications related to the terminal diagnosis, medical equipment (hospital bed, oxygen, wheelchair), home health aide visits, social work, chaplain services, and bereavement counseling — most at $0 to the patient or family.

Pennsylvania Medicaid (Medical Assistance) covers hospice through the HealthChoices managed care program, which operates across five geographic zones. Plans vary by region: UPMC Health Plan and Highmark (Gateway Health) are primary in western PA; Independence Blue Cross and Aetna Better Health are primary in southeastern PA; Geisinger Health Plan covers central PA. Pennsylvania also operates the LIFE (Living Independence for the Elderly) program, which provides home- and community-based services to nursing-home-eligible individuals.

Pennsylvania licenses and regulates hospice providers through the Pennsylvania Department of Health. All Medicare-certified Pennsylvania hospice providers meet state licensing requirements as a condition of federal certification.

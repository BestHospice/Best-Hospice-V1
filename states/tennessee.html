Finding Hospice Care in Tennessee

Tennessee is home to more than 7 million residents and a senior population growing rapidly across both urban centers and rural counties. The state spans dramatically different healthcare landscapes — from the large academic medical systems of Nashville and Memphis to community hospitals in Knoxville, Chattanooga, and Jackson, extending to rural Appalachian communities in East Tennessee and agricultural counties in West Tennessee where access to specialized care can require more planning.

Hospice Care in Nashville

The Nashville metropolitan area — covering Davidson, Williamson, Rutherford, Wilson, Sumner, and surrounding counties — is Tennessee's largest and fastest-growing hospice market. The region's senior population is expanding rapidly alongside overall population growth, driven by migration into Middle Tennessee from across the country.

Major Nashville health systems that coordinate hospice transitions include Vanderbilt University Medical Center (the state's primary academic medical center), Ascension Saint Thomas (Saint Thomas Midtown, Saint Thomas West, Saint Thomas Rutherford), TriStar Health / HCA Healthcare (Centennial Medical Center, Skyline Medical Center, Southern Hills), and Nashville General Hospital (the safety-net hospital serving uninsured and Medicaid patients). Each system has palliative care and social work teams that facilitate hospice referrals at discharge.

When choosing a Nashville hospice provider, confirm the coverage area for your specific suburb. Providers based in Brentwood or Franklin may not routinely serve Antioch or Hermitage, and agencies focused on Murfreesboro or Smyrna may have longer response times in Hendersonville or Gallatin.

Hospice Care in Knoxville, Chattanooga, and Memphis

Knoxville and East Tennessee are anchored by the University of Tennessee Medical Center (the primary academic medical center for the region) and Covenant Health (Parkwest, Fort Sanders Regional, Methodist Medical Center of Oak Ridge). The University of Tennessee's medical school and Knoxville's large retiree population — many drawn by the Smoky Mountains and lower cost of living — create consistent demand for hospice services across Knox, Blount, Anderson, Sevier, and surrounding counties.

Chattanooga is served primarily by Erlanger Health System (the region's Level I trauma center and academic affiliate of the University of Tennessee College of Medicine Chattanooga) and CHI Memorial (now Mercy Health). Hospice providers in the Chattanooga area typically serve Hamilton, Bradley, Catoosa (Georgia), and Walker (Georgia) counties — a cross-state service area that requires confirming your provider's geographic reach before enrollment.

Memphis is home to Methodist Le Bonheur Healthcare, Baptist Memorial Health Care, Saint Francis Hospital (Tenet), and Regional One Health — the region's safety-net hospital. Memphis's large African American community, significant uninsured population, and high rates of cardiovascular disease and diabetes create a distinct patient profile that many hospice providers have experience navigating. Memphis providers typically serve Shelby, Fayette, Tipton, and DeSoto (Mississippi) counties.

Medicare and TennCare Coverage

Medicare Part A covers hospice for eligible Tennessee patients with a physician-certified prognosis of six months or less. In 2026, covered services include nursing visits, comfort medications for the terminal diagnosis, medical equipment (hospital bed, oxygen, wheelchair), home health aide, social work, chaplain services, and bereavement counseling — most at $0 to the patient or family.

TennCare, Tennessee's Medicaid program, covers hospice for qualifying low-income residents through three managed care organizations: BlueCare Tennessee, UnitedHealthcare Community Plan of Tennessee, and Amerigroup Tennessee. Medicare Advantage plans from Humana, Aetna, UnitedHealthcare, and BlueCross BlueShield of Tennessee are widely available statewide — all must cover hospice at Medicare Part A levels. Verify your specific plan with the provider.

Tennessee licenses and regulates hospice providers through the Tennessee Department of Health's Division of Health Care Facilities. All Medicare-certified Tennessee hospice providers meet state licensing requirements as a condition of federal certification.

Tennessee Hospice Laws, Licensing & Benefits

Certificate of Need (CON) — Important for Tennessee Families: Tennessee requires a Certificate of Need for hospice licensure. The number of licensed hospice providers per service area is regulated by the state — confirm that any provider you're considering holds an active Tennessee CON for your county before enrolling. CON regulation can limit provider choice in some Tennessee communities.

Advance Directive Form: Tennessee uses the Tennessee Advance Care Plan (combining a living will and healthcare agent designation) to document end-of-life preferences. Tennessee also uses the POLST (Physician Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment) form as a portable medical order for patients with serious illness. Hospice teams can help families complete or update these documents when starting care.

Medicaid Home Care Programs: Tennessee Medicaid (TennCare) offers CHOICES in Home and Community Based Services, which provides home care services for qualifying low-income seniors and adults with physical disabilities as an alternative to nursing facility placement. CHOICES is operated through TennCare managed care organizations. ECF CHOICES expands eligibility for adults with physical disabilities. Dual-eligible patients should confirm how their TennCare managed care plan interacts with Medicare hospice benefits.

Hospice Licensing Authority: Tennessee licenses hospice providers through the Tennessee Department of Health, Division of Health Care Facilities. All Medicare-certified Tennessee hospice providers meet state licensing requirements as a condition of federal certification.

Free Medicare Counseling: Tennessee's SHIP program (administered through the Tennessee Commission on Aging and Disability) offers free Medicare counseling statewide. SHIP counselors can help families understand hospice benefits, CON provider availability, TennCare CHOICES coordination with hospice, and Medicare Advantage plans in Nashville, Memphis, Knoxville, and Chattanooga.

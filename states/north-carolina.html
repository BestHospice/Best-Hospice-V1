North Carolina is one of the fastest-growing states in the South, with a senior population expanding rapidly across its major metros and retirement communities in the mountains and coast. Hospice Care in Charlotte

Charlotte is the largest city in North Carolina and one of the fastest-growing metros in the Southeast. The Charlotte region — covering Mecklenburg, Union, Cabarrus, Gaston, and surrounding counties — has a substantial and rapidly growing senior population driven by in-migration from across the country.

Major Charlotte health systems that coordinate hospice transitions include Atrium Health (one of the largest nonprofit health systems in the Southeast, operating Carolinas Medical Center and dozens of facilities across the Carolinas), Novant Health (Presbyterian Medical Center and a large network of clinics throughout the Charlotte metro), and CommonSpirit Health. Atrium Health's Levine Cancer Institute has an integrated palliative care program that works closely with hospice partners for oncology patients.

Hospice Care in Raleigh-Durham and the Research Triangle

The Research Triangle — Raleigh, Durham, and Chapel Hill — is anchored by one of the country's premier academic medical ecosystems. UNC Health (the clinical enterprise of the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, including UNC Hospitals and UNC Rex Health Care) and Duke Health (Duke University Hospital, Duke Regional, and Duke Raleigh) both have highly regarded palliative care programs and facilitate hospice referrals across the Triangle.

WakeMed Health and Hospitals serves Wake County and the eastern Triangle. The region's concentration of research universities and medical institutions means that families in the Triangle often have access to providers with strong clinical expertise and experience managing complex diagnoses. Confirm service area coverage by ZIP code before selecting a provider — the Triangle's rapid growth has created significant variation in which agencies serve which neighborhoods.

Medicare and North Carolina Medicaid Coverage

Medicare Part A covers hospice for eligible North Carolina patients with a physician-certified prognosis of six months or less. In 2026, covered services include nursing visits, comfort medications for the terminal diagnosis, medical equipment (hospital bed, oxygen, wheelchair), home health aide, social work, chaplain services, and bereavement counseling — most at $0 to the patient or family.

North Carolina Medicaid covers hospice for qualifying low-income residents. NC transitioned to managed care in 2021 — Standard Plans for physical health are operated by Aetna Better Health of NC, AmeriHealth Caritas NC, Healthy Blue (Blue Cross NC), UnitedHealthcare Community Plan of NC, and WellCare of NC. Medicare Advantage plans are widely available in Charlotte, Raleigh-Durham, and the Triad — all must cover hospice at Medicare Part A levels.

North Carolina licenses and regulates hospice providers through the NC Division of Health Service Regulation (DHSR). All Medicare-certified NC hospice providers meet state licensing requirements as a condition of federal certification. The state's MOST (Medical Orders for Scope of Treatment) form functions like a POLST and travels with the patient to communicate end-of-life wishes — hospice teams can assist families in completing or updating these documents.

North Carolina Hospice Laws, Licensing & Benefits

Certificate of Need (CON): North Carolina does not require a Certificate of Need for hospice licensure. Any provider that meets state licensing and Medicare certification requirements may operate in North Carolina, giving families broad provider choices across Charlotte, the Research Triangle, the Triad, Asheville, and communities statewide.

Advance Directive Form — MOST: North Carolina uses the MOST (Medical Orders for Scope of Treatment) form as its portable medical order for patients with serious illness. The MOST is similar to a POLST and travels with the patient to communicate end-of-life wishes to all healthcare providers, including hospice teams, emergency responders, and hospitals. North Carolina also uses a Health Care Power of Attorney and Declaration of a Desire for a Natural Death (living will). Hospice teams can help families complete or update these documents.

Medicaid Home Care Programs: North Carolina Medicaid offers the Community Alternatives Program for Disabled Adults (CAP/DA), which provides home and community-based services to qualifying low-income adults as an alternative to nursing facility placement. NC Medicaid transitioned to managed care in 2021 — Standard Plans administer CAP/DA and other waiver services. Dual-eligible patients should confirm how their NC Medicaid managed care plan interacts with Medicare hospice benefits.

Hospice Licensing Authority: North Carolina licenses hospice providers through the NC Division of Health Service Regulation (DHSR), Health Care Personnel and Facilities Regulation Section. All Medicare-certified NC hospice providers meet state licensing requirements as a condition of federal certification.

Free Medicare Counseling: North Carolina's SHIIP (Seniors' Health Insurance Information Program), administered by the NC Department of Insurance, offers free Medicare counseling across the state. SHIIP counselors can help families understand hospice benefits, MOST form requirements, NC Medicaid managed care coordination, and Medicare Advantage plans in Charlotte, Raleigh, and other NC markets.

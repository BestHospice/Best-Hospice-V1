Finding Hospice and Home Care in North Carolina

North Carolina has a growing and well-regarded hospice community, with established providers serving major metros including Charlotte, Raleigh-Durham, Greensboro, Winston-Salem, and Asheville, as well as smaller communities throughout the Piedmont, coastal plain, and western mountain regions. The state has seen strong growth in its senior population, increasing demand for quality end-of-life care across all regions.

Medicare and Medicaid in North Carolina

Medicare covers hospice in North Carolina for eligible beneficiaries with a physician-certified terminal diagnosis and a prognosis of six months or less. North Carolina Medicaid also covers hospice services for qualifying enrollees. NC Medicaid's Community Alternatives Program for Disabled Adults (CAP/DA) provides home- and community-based services as an alternative to nursing home care for eligible individuals.

North Carolina completed its transition to Medicaid managed care (Tailored Plans and Standard Plans) in recent years. Families should confirm how a loved one's current plan interacts with hospice enrollment. Hospice providers can help navigate the enrollment process and confirm coverage details.

Hospice Care in North Carolina: What Families Should Know

North Carolina hospice agencies are licensed by the NC Division of Health Service Regulation (DHSR). Medicare-certified hospices are also subject to federal Conditions of Participation and CMS oversight. Families can review quality data and inspection history through the DHSR website and Medicare's Care Compare tool.

North Carolina recognizes both healthcare powers of attorney and advance directives for natural death (living wills). The state also has a standardized MOST (Medical Orders for Scope of Treatment) form that functions similarly to a POLST, traveling with the patient to communicate end-of-life wishes. The hospice team can assist families in completing or updating these documents.

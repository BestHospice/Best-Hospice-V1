Finding Hospice and Home Care in Michigan

Michigan has a well-developed hospice and home care sector serving communities across the Lower Peninsula and Upper Peninsula. Major markets include Detroit and its suburbs, Grand Rapids, Lansing, Flint, Kalamazoo, and Ann Arbor. More rural areas of northern Michigan and the Upper Peninsula may have fewer agency options, making it particularly valuable for families to verify which providers serve their specific area.

Best Hospice and Home Health helps Michigan families compare verified providers by city, review coverage areas, and connect with local agencies. Use the search tool to find hospice and home care support near you.

Medicare and Medicaid in Michigan

Medicare covers hospice in Michigan for eligible beneficiaries with a physician-certified terminal diagnosis. Michigan Medicaid (MI Health Link and Healthy Michigan Plan) also covers hospice services for qualifying enrollees. Michigan's MI Choice Waiver program provides home- and community-based long-term care services as an alternative to nursing home placement.

Families with loved ones enrolled in Medicaid or managed care plans should confirm with the hospice provider how enrollment interacts with current coverage. In most cases, the Medicare Hospice Benefit becomes the primary payer and Medicaid covers additional costs not paid by Medicare.

Hospice Care in Michigan: What Families Should Know

Michigan hospice providers are licensed by the Michigan Department of Health and Human Services (MDHHS). Medicare-certified hospices are also subject to federal Conditions of Participation and CMS oversight. Families can review hospice quality data through the Medicare Care Compare tool at Medicare.gov.

Michigan law allows individuals to designate a patient advocate to make healthcare decisions on their behalf through a Patient Advocate Designation (PAD) document. The hospice team can help families confirm that existing advance directives are on file and that the patient's wishes are reflected in the care plan.

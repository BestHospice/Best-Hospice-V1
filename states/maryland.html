Finding Hospice Care in Maryland

Maryland's compact geography and proximity to Washington, D.C., make it one of the most densely served hospice markets on the East Coast. The Baltimore-Washington corridor — spanning Baltimore City, Baltimore County, Howard County, Montgomery County, Prince George's County, and Anne Arundel County — contains the highest concentration of providers, but quality options also exist in Frederick, Annapolis, and the Eastern Shore.

BestHospice.com helps Maryland families find verified hospice providers by ZIP code, compare services, and connect with local agencies without sales pressure or referral fees.

Hospice Care in Baltimore and the DC Suburbs

Baltimore is anchored by one of the world's leading academic medical systems: Johns Hopkins Medicine, with Johns Hopkins Hospital serving as the primary center for complex cancer and cardiac referrals. The University of Maryland Medical System (UMMS) operates University of Maryland Medical Center, Maryland Shock Trauma, and hospitals across the state. Sinai Hospital of Maryland (LifeBridge Health) and Mercy Medical Center also serve Baltimore's diverse neighborhoods. Each system has palliative care teams that coordinate hospice transitions.

Montgomery County and Prince George's County — the Maryland suburbs immediately adjacent to D.C. — are among the most populated jurisdictions in the state. MedStar Washington Hospital Center and Holy Cross Health (Trinity Health) are major referral centers for this corridor. Silver Spring, Rockville, Germantown, and Capital Heights each have active hospice provider coverage given the population density.

Medicare and Maryland Medicaid Coverage

Medicare Part A covers hospice for eligible Maryland patients with a physician-certified prognosis of six months or less. Covered services include nursing visits, comfort medications related to the terminal diagnosis, medical equipment (hospital bed, oxygen, wheelchair), home health aide visits, social work, chaplain services, and bereavement counseling — most at $0 to the patient or family.

Maryland Medicaid covers hospice through the HealthChoice managed care program. Participating plans include Aetna Better Health of Maryland, CareFirst Community Health Plan Maryland, Jai Medical Systems, Maryland Physicians Care, MedStar Family Choice, Priority Partners (Johns Hopkins), and UnitedHealthcare Community Plan of Maryland. Dual-eligible patients may coordinate Medicare and Medicaid benefits. Medicare Advantage plans from Kaiser Permanente, Aetna, UnitedHealthcare, and Humana are widely available in Maryland and must cover hospice at Medicare Part A levels.

Maryland licenses hospice providers through the Maryland Department of Health (MDH), Office of Health Care Quality. All Medicare-certified Maryland hospice providers also meet state licensing requirements as a condition of federal certification.

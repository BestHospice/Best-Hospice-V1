Does Medicare Cover Hospice Care?

Yes — and more than most families realize. Here is exactly what Medicare pays for, who qualifies, and what to expect.

Short answer: Yes. Medicare Part A covers hospice care for eligible patients at little to no out-of-pocket cost. Most core hospice services — nursing, medications for symptom relief, medical equipment, social work, and counseling — are fully covered.

What is the Medicare Hospice Benefit?

The Medicare Hospice Benefit is a program under Medicare Part A that covers end-of-life comfort care for people with a terminal illness. When a patient elects hospice, they agree to focus care on comfort and quality of life rather than curative treatment for their terminal diagnosis.

Hospice care can be provided at home, in a nursing facility, in an assisted living facility, or in a dedicated hospice inpatient facility. Medicare covers the hospice services regardless of where the patient lives.

The benefit was created specifically to reduce the financial and logistical burden on families during one of life's most difficult periods. Understanding it fully can help your family make informed decisions quickly when it matters most.

What Does Medicare Cover for Hospice Care?

Medicare hospice coverage includes a comprehensive set of services. Here is a complete breakdown:

Medical Services

  • Physician services — visits and oversight from the hospice medical director and the patient's attending physician
  • Nursing care — regular visits from registered nurses to manage symptoms, assess pain, and coordinate care
  • Nurse practitioner services — as the attending clinician of record in eligible cases

Medications

  • Prescription drugs for symptom control — medications related to the terminal diagnosis are covered (pain management, anti-nausea, anxiety, etc.)
  • Note: Medicare may charge a small copay of up to $5 per prescription for outpatient drugs under the hospice benefit
  • Medications unrelated to the terminal diagnosis are generally not covered under the hospice benefit but may be covered by Medicare Part D

Equipment and Supplies

  • Hospital beds and mattresses
  • Wheelchairs and walkers
  • Oxygen equipment
  • Bedside commodes
  • Wound care and medical supplies

Support Services

  • Home health aide and homemaker services — help with bathing, dressing, grooming, and light housekeeping
  • Social worker services — help navigating financial, legal, and emotional challenges
  • Counseling — including dietary counseling and mental health support
  • Spiritual care — chaplain services for patients and families of any or no faith background
  • Volunteer support — trained volunteers for companionship and respite

Respite Care

  • Short-term inpatient care so that caregivers can rest — up to 5 consecutive days at a time
  • Medicare covers 95% of the respite care rate; families may pay a 5% cost-share

Bereavement Support

  • Grief counseling and bereavement services for family members — covered for up to 13 months after the patient's death

Medicare Hospice Eligibility Requirements

To qualify for Medicare hospice benefits, all of the following must be true:

  1. Enrolled in Medicare Part A — the patient must have Medicare Part A coverage
  2. Terminal diagnosis — a physician must certify that the patient has a terminal illness with a life expectancy of 6 months or less if the disease runs its normal course
  3. Two-physician certification — both the hospice medical director and the patient's attending physician must certify eligibility
  4. Elect the hospice benefit — the patient (or their legal representative) must sign a statement choosing comfort-focused care over curative treatment for the terminal diagnosis
  5. Medicare-certified provider — care must be provided by a Medicare-certified hospice organization
Important: Electing hospice does not mean giving up all medical treatment. Patients can still receive treatment for conditions unrelated to their terminal diagnosis. And they can choose to leave hospice at any time and return to curative care if they change their mind.

How Long Does Medicare Cover Hospice?

Medicare hospice coverage is organized into benefit periods with physician recertification at each stage. There is no lifetime limit on the number of benefit periods a patient can receive.

Benefit Period Length Recertification Required
First period 90 days At the end of 90 days
Second period 90 days At the end of 90 days
Subsequent periods 60 days each At the end of each 60-day period

At each recertification, the hospice physician must document that the patient still meets eligibility criteria. If the patient's condition improves and they no longer qualify, they are discharged from hospice — but they can re-enroll later if their condition declines again.

If a patient lives longer than 6 months: Coverage continues as long as eligibility is recertified. Living longer than the initial prognosis does not disqualify a patient from the benefit.

What Does Medicare NOT Cover in Hospice?

While Medicare hospice coverage is broad, there are some notable exclusions families should know:

  • Room and board — if the patient is in a nursing home or assisted living, Medicare hospice does not cover the daily room-and-board cost. The patient or family pays this separately.
  • Curative treatment for the terminal diagnosis — once hospice is elected, Medicare will not pay for treatments aimed at curing the terminal illness (such as chemotherapy for the terminal cancer). However, curative treatment for other conditions is still covered.
  • Emergency room visits and hospitalizations — for issues related to the terminal diagnosis, unless arranged by the hospice team. Unrelated emergencies may still be covered by Medicare Part A/B.
  • Medications unrelated to the terminal diagnosis — though these may be covered by Medicare Part D.

Frequently Asked Questions About Medicare and Hospice

Does Medicare Part A or Part B cover hospice?

Hospice is covered under Medicare Part A. You do not need Part B for hospice services, though Part B may cover conditions unrelated to your terminal diagnosis.

Does Medicare cover hospice in a nursing home?

Medicare covers the hospice services provided in a nursing home — nursing visits, medications, counseling, and equipment. However, Medicare hospice does not cover the nursing home's room-and-board costs. The patient or family pays room and board separately (Medicaid may help if the patient qualifies).

Can a patient leave hospice and go back to regular Medicare coverage?

Yes. Patients can revoke the hospice benefit at any time. When they do, they return to regular Medicare coverage and can receive curative treatment again. They can also re-enroll in hospice later if they again meet eligibility criteria.

Does the patient have to stop seeing their regular doctor?

No. Patients can continue seeing their attending physician. The hospice team works alongside the patient's regular doctor to manage care.

What is the Medicare hospice criteria for a 6-month prognosis?

The 6-month prognosis means that if the illness runs its normal course, the patient is expected to live 6 months or less. This is a physician's clinical judgment — not an exact deadline. Patients who live longer than 6 months do not lose coverage as long as they continue to meet eligibility criteria at each recertification.

Does Medicare cover hospice care at home?

Yes. Medicare hospice is most commonly provided in the patient's home. The hospice team comes to the patient rather than requiring them to travel to a facility.

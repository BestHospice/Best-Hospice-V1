Hospice Care in the Chicago Metro

Chicago is the third-largest city in the United States and home to one of the most comprehensive healthcare ecosystems in the country. The Chicago metro — spanning Cook, DuPage, Lake, Kane, Will, and McHenry counties — has a large and competitive hospice market, with providers ranging from large national organizations to independent community agencies serving specific neighborhoods and suburbs.

Because Chicago's hospice market is so large, service territories can vary significantly between agencies. A provider licensed in Cook County may not actively staff teams in your specific suburb or neighborhood. Chicago's Major Health Systems and Hospice Transitions

Most hospice transitions in Chicago begin with a referral from a hospital discharge planner, palliative care team, or oncologist. Chicago's major health systems each have palliative care programs that coordinate hospice referrals:

Northwestern Medicine is anchored by Northwestern Memorial Hospital in Streeterville and the Robert H. Lurie Comprehensive Cancer Center, a National Cancer Institute-designated Comprehensive Cancer Center. Northwestern's palliative care program manages complex cancer and cardiac transitions across the system's extensive North Shore and suburban hospitals.

University of Chicago Medicine operates its flagship hospital in Hyde Park and is affiliated with the Comprehensive Cancer Center at UChicago Medicine. Its palliative care and social work teams coordinate hospice transitions for patients on the South Side and surrounding communities.

Rush University Medical Center is a large academic medical center in the Medical District of Chicago with a strong palliative care program. Loyola University Medical Center (Trinity Health) in Maywood serves the western suburbs and coordinates hospice transitions for cardiac and oncology patients across the western Cook County corridor.

Advocate Aurora Health is the largest private hospital network in Illinois, with hospitals across the metro including Advocate Christ Medical Center in Oak Lawn, Advocate Lutheran General Hospital in Park Ridge, Advocate Sherman Hospital in Elgin, and Advocate Good Samaritan Hospital in Downers Grove. Cook County Health (Stroger Hospital) is the public safety-net system coordinating hospice for uninsured and Medicaid patients in the urban core.

Where Hospice Care Is Delivered in Chicago

The majority of Chicago-area hospice patients receive care at home — in a private residence, apartment, or family member's home. Nurses and aides travel to the patient's location, typically visiting multiple times per week based on need. Chicago's dense urban core and expansive suburban footprint means providers may specialize in specific geographic zones; confirming active service in your neighborhood before enrolling is important.

Chicago has a significant number of assisted living facilities, memory care communities, and skilled nursing facilities whose residents receive hospice from an outside agency. Many of Chicago's older communities — particularly on the North and Northwest sides — also have multi-generational households where hospice care supports family caregivers in the home.

Chicago's large and diverse population — including significant Polish, Mexican, Puerto Rican, Chinese, Korean, Filipino, Indian, and African American communities — means multilingual hospice services are frequently available. Ask about language capabilities and cultural experience when evaluating providers.

Medicare and Illinois Medicaid Coverage in Chicago

Medicare Part A covers hospice for eligible Chicago-area patients with a physician-certified prognosis of six months or less. Covered services include nursing visits, comfort medications for the terminal diagnosis, medical equipment (hospital bed, oxygen, wheelchair), home health aide, social work, chaplain services, and bereavement counseling — most at $0 to the patient or family.

Illinois Medicaid covers hospice through the HealthChoice Illinois managed care program. Plans serving the Chicago metro include Aetna Better Health of Illinois, Blue Cross Community Health Plans, CountyCare (Cook County Health), Harmony Health Plan, IlliniCare Health (Centene), Meridian Health Plan of Illinois, Molina Healthcare of Illinois, and NextLevel Health Partners. Medicare Advantage plans from Humana, UnitedHealthcare, Aetna, and Blue Cross Blue Shield of Illinois are widely available — all must cover hospice at Medicare Part A levels.

Illinois licenses hospice providers through the Illinois Department of Public Health (IDPH). All Medicare-certified Illinois hospice providers meet state licensing requirements as a condition of federal certification. Families can review inspection results through the IDPH website and quality data through Medicare's Care Compare tool.

How to Choose a Hospice Provider in Chicago

Chicago's large hospice market means families have real choices — and real differences between providers. These are the questions that matter most:

  • Do they serve your specific ZIP code? Coverage zones in Chicago can be hyperlocal. Confirm the provider is actively staffed in your neighborhood or suburb before scheduling an intake call.
  • How quickly can they respond? Ask about intake turnaround and first visit timing — especially for urgent hospital discharge situations.
  • What is their experience with your loved one's diagnosis? Cancer, dementia, heart failure, COPD, and ALS each require different symptom management expertise.
  • What languages do they serve? Chicago's multilingual communities have providers who can communicate in Spanish, Polish, Mandarin, Tagalog, Korean, and other languages. Confirm availability.
  • Do they have an inpatient arrangement? If symptoms escalate beyond what can be managed at home, where would your loved one receive inpatient care?

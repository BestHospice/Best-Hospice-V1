Hospice Care in the Columbus Metro

Columbus is the largest city in Ohio and one of the fastest-growing major metros in the Midwest. The Columbus metro — spanning Franklin County and surrounding counties including Delaware, Fairfield, Licking, Union, and Pickaway — has a well-developed hospice sector serving communities from the city's urban core to rapidly growing outer suburbs like Dublin, Westerville, Gahanna, Hilliard, Grove City, and Upper Arlington.

Ohio is consistently among the top states for hospice utilization, and Columbus reflects that culture — with strong community awareness of hospice and a provider market that includes both large national organizations and locally rooted nonprofits. Columbus's Major Health Systems and Hospice Transitions

OhioHealth is the largest nonprofit hospital system in central Ohio, operating Riverside Methodist Hospital (the flagship), Grant Medical Center, Doctors Hospital, Dublin Methodist Hospital, and other facilities across the metro. OhioHealth has an extensive palliative care program and is one of the primary sources of hospice referrals in central Ohio.

Mount Carmel Health System (Trinity Health) operates Mount Carmel St. Ann's in Westerville, Mount Carmel East, Mount Carmel West, and Mount Carmel New Albany. Trinity Health's Catholic mission aligns closely with hospice values of dignity and comfort, and Mount Carmel's palliative care teams coordinate end-of-life transitions across their system.

The Ohio State University Wexner Medical Center is Ohio's academic medical flagship, with the James Cancer Hospital and Solove Research Institute serving as a national cancer referral center. The James and OSU's palliative care program coordinate hospice transitions for complex oncology and specialty patients from across the state. Nationwide Children's Hospital, while primarily a pediatric center, also coordinates pediatric palliative care and occasionally adult complex cases.

Medicare and Ohio Medicaid Coverage in Columbus

Medicare Part A covers hospice for eligible Columbus-area patients with a physician-certified prognosis of six months or less. Covered services include nursing visits, comfort medications, medical equipment, home health aide, social work, chaplain services, and bereavement counseling — most at $0 to the patient or family.

Ohio Medicaid covers hospice for qualifying residents. MyCare Ohio coordinates care for dual-eligible individuals through managed care plans including Aetna Better Health of Ohio, Buckeye Health Plan (Centene), CareSource, Molina Healthcare of Ohio, and UnitedHealthcare Community Plan of Ohio. Ohio's PASSPORT program provides home- and community-based services for older adults as an alternative to nursing facility placement. Medicare Advantage plans from UnitedHealthcare, Humana, Aetna, and Medical Mutual of Ohio are widely available in the Columbus area — all must cover hospice at Medicare Part A levels.

Ohio licenses hospice providers through the Ohio Department of Health. Families can review provider survey results through the Ohio Department of Health website and Medicare's Care Compare tool.

How to Choose a Hospice Provider in Columbus

  • Do they serve your specific ZIP code? Franklin County is well-served, but outer suburbs in Delaware, Licking, and Fairfield counties may have fewer options. Confirm active service.
  • How quickly can they respond? Especially for hospital discharges from OhioHealth, Mount Carmel, or the OSU Wexner Medical Center.
  • What is their experience with your loved one's diagnosis? Cancer, dementia, and heart failure each require different clinical expertise.
  • Are they nonprofit or for-profit? Columbus has strong nonprofit hospice options — some families prefer mission-driven agencies.
  • Do they have an inpatient arrangement? Confirm where inpatient care would be provided if symptoms escalate.

Finding hospice care in San Antonio

San Antonio is the second-largest city in Texas and the seventh-largest in the United States, with more than 1.4 million residents in the city and over 2.5 million in the greater Bexar County metro area. It is home to a large and growing senior population, a multigenerational Hispanic/Latino community that comprises approximately 64% of the population, and an extraordinarily large military presence — making it one of the most distinctive hospice markets in the country.

San Antonio has a competitive hospice market with providers ranging from large national and regional organizations to smaller locally-owned agencies. Spanish-language services and culturally responsive care for Hispanic and Latino families are widely available and represent a core expectation — not an exception — among local providers. Families navigating hospice after care at University Health, Baptist Health System, CHRISTUS Santa Rosa, or Methodist Healthcare will typically find that hospital social workers and palliative care teams have working relationships with multiple local hospice agencies and can offer referrals.

Key questions to ask San Antonio hospice providers before enrolling:

  • What is your after-hours nursing response time — how quickly does a nurse arrive?
  • What is your current nurse-to-patient ratio?
  • Do you cover my specific ZIP code, including communities outside Bexar County?
  • Do you have bilingual (English/Spanish) nurses, social workers, and chaplains?
  • Do you accept Medicare, STAR+PLUS Medicaid, VA benefits, and/or TRICARE?
  • What is your experience with my loved one's specific diagnosis?
  • Are you licensed by Texas HHSC and Medicare-certified?

San Antonio hospice coverage area

San Antonio hospice providers typically serve Bexar County and often extend into surrounding counties. The broader San Antonio metropolitan area includes communities that vary considerably in distance from the city center — confirm your specific location before enrolling. Common coverage communities include:

  • San Antonio (all quadrants — North San Antonio, South Side, West Side, East Side, Stone Oak, Alamo Heights, Olmos Park)
  • New Braunfels (Comal County) — a fast-growing suburb northeast of San Antonio
  • Seguin (Guadalupe County)
  • Schertz, Cibolo, Converse, Universal City (Guadalupe/Bexar counties)
  • Helotes, Leon Valley, Lackland AFB area (west Bexar County)
  • Boerne (Kendall County) — confirm coverage
  • Pleasanton, Jourdanton (Atascosa County) — confirm coverage
  • Castroville, Hondo (Medina County) — confirm coverage

Providers based in central San Antonio may not cover all rural communities in surrounding counties. If your family member lives in Comal, Kendall, Medina, or Atascosa County, explicitly confirm the provider's service area for that location before enrollment.

San Antonio health systems and hospice transitions

Many San Antonio hospice enrollments begin after a hospitalization or oncology treatment. Case managers, social workers, and palliative care teams at major San Antonio hospitals coordinate hospice transitions when curative treatment is no longer the appropriate goal. Key San Antonio health systems include:

  • University Health (formerly University Health System) — Bexar County's public academic medical center, operated in partnership with UT Health San Antonio. University Hospital is the region's Level I trauma center and primary safety-net hospital. UT Health San Antonio MD Anderson Cancer Center — part of the MD Anderson network — provides oncology and palliative care services and is a common entry point for hospice referrals among cancer patients in South Texas. If your family member is receiving care at University Health, ask the social work or palliative care team to help identify an appropriate hospice provider.
  • Baptist Health System (Tenet Healthcare) — One of the largest hospital networks in San Antonio, operating Baptist Medical Center, North Central Baptist Hospital, Northeast Baptist Hospital, Mission Trail Baptist Hospital, and St. Luke's Baptist Hospital. Baptist Health System case managers are experienced coordinating community hospice transitions across all five campuses.
  • CHRISTUS Santa Rosa Health System — A Catholic nonprofit system with a mission-driven approach to end-of-life care reflecting its Catholic healthcare heritage. CHRISTUS Santa Rosa operates multiple locations including downtown San Antonio and New Braunfels, making it a common referral source for hospice in both the urban core and the northern suburbs. CHRISTUS's palliative care program aligns with its values around dignity and spiritual care.
  • Methodist Healthcare (HCA Healthcare) — Operates Methodist Hospital, Metropolitan Methodist Hospital, Texsan Methodist Hospital, and Stone Oak Methodist Hospital across San Antonio. Methodist Healthcare's case management teams coordinate hospice referrals across these campuses, which collectively serve a large portion of the north and central San Antonio population.

If a palliative care or hospice conversation has not been initiated by the hospital team, families may ask directly: "Can we speak with a social worker or palliative care team about whether hospice is appropriate?" You may also contact a San Antonio hospice provider directly — they can assess eligibility independently of the hospital.

Military families and veterans: hospice in San Antonio

San Antonio's military dimension is unlike any other city in the country. Joint Base San Antonio (JBSA) — the largest joint military installation in the United States — encompasses Lackland Air Force Base, Fort Sam Houston, and Randolph Air Force Base, together supporting over 80,000 active-duty military personnel, civilian employees, and their families. The veteran population across Bexar County and South Texas is among the largest in the nation. This creates two distinct healthcare access situations that affect hospice decisions:

  • Brooke Army Medical Center (BAMC) at Fort Sam Houston is the largest military hospital in the United States and a major referral and treatment center for active-duty military, retirees, and their dependents. Patients receiving care at BAMC who are approaching end of life should consult with BAMC's palliative care team and case managers about hospice options — either within the military system or through a community provider.
  • South Texas Veterans Health Care System (VA San Antonio) provides healthcare to veterans across San Antonio and South Texas. The VA provides hospice benefits to eligible veterans, either through VA-employed staff or through contracted community hospice providers. Veterans enrolled in VA healthcare should contact their VA primary care team or patient advocate to discuss VA hospice eligibility and options. Veterans who are also Medicare-eligible can access community hospice under Medicare and coordinate with the VA for supplemental support.
  • TRICARE coverage: Active-duty family members and military retirees covered by TRICARE may have hospice benefits under their TRICARE plan. TRICARE's hospice benefit mirrors Medicare's in structure, but specific coverage details depend on the plan type (TRICARE Prime, TRICARE Select, TRICARE for Life). Ask prospective hospice providers whether they accept TRICARE and have experience coordinating with military insurance. TRICARE for Life — the Medicare supplement for military retirees — works alongside Medicare hospice benefits.

San Antonio hospice providers with experience serving the military community often have staff familiar with JBSA, BAMC, and VA systems. When evaluating providers, ask specifically about their experience with TRICARE billing and VA coordination if this applies to your family.

Medicare and Texas Medicaid coverage in San Antonio

Medicare Part A covers hospice comprehensively for eligible San Antonio patients in 2026:

  • Nursing visits — $0
  • Comfort medications related to the terminal diagnosis — $0 or up to $5 per prescription
  • Medical equipment (hospital bed, oxygen concentrator, wheelchair, bedside commode) — $0
  • Home health aide and homemaker services — $0
  • Social worker, chaplain, and bereavement counseling — $0
  • Inpatient respite care — 5% coinsurance

Texas STAR+PLUS Medicaid covers hospice for qualifying low-income San Antonio-area residents. STAR+PLUS is Texas's Medicaid managed care program for adults with disabilities and elderly Texans, and it includes home and community-based services relevant to end-of-life care. Managed care organizations serving Bexar County include Aetna Better Health of Texas, Humana Healthy Horizons in Texas, Molina Healthcare of Texas, Superior HealthPlan (Centene), and UnitedHealthcare Community Plan of Texas. Availability varies by plan and region — confirm STAR+PLUS enrollment and interaction with the hospice benefit before starting care.

Texas also offers the Community Based Alternatives (CBA) waiver, providing home- and community-based services as an alternative to nursing facility placement for dual-eligible and Medicaid-eligible individuals. Families of patients already enrolled in CBA or similar waivers should ask the hospice provider how enrollment interacts with their current Medicaid services.

Medicare Advantage plans — including those from UnitedHealthcare, Humana, Aetna, and Blue Cross Blue Shield of Texas — are widely used in Bexar County and must cover hospice at least at Medicare Part A levels. Verify your specific plan's hospice provisions with the provider.

Texas's SHIP program — administered through the Texas Health and Human Services Commission — provides free Medicare counseling through trained volunteer counselors. SHIP counselors can explain how STAR+PLUS and Medicare interact with hospice, and can help families evaluate Medicare Advantage plan hospice coverage in San Antonio. Contact Texas HHSC to locate a SHIP counselor near you.

Texas laws and regulations affecting hospice in San Antonio

No Certificate of Need requirement: Texas does not require a Certificate of Need (CON) for hospice licensure. Any provider that meets Texas state licensing requirements and achieves Medicare certification may operate in San Antonio. This means the market is open to competition, and families may find a wider range of provider options than in states with CON restrictions. The openness of the market also means families should verify licensure and Medicare certification before enrolling.

Hospice licensing authority: Texas licenses hospice providers through the Texas Health and Human Services Commission (HHSC). All Medicare-certified Texas hospice providers meet HHSC licensing requirements as a condition of federal certification. Families can verify a provider's Texas license status through HHSC's online provider search.

Advance directive forms in Texas: Texas uses specific legal forms to document end-of-life care preferences that differ from those used in other states:

  • Directive to Physicians, Family, or Surrogates (Texas Living Will) — A written document stating the patient's wishes regarding life-sustaining treatment when they have a terminal or irreversible condition. This is the Texas equivalent of a living will and is recognized under the Texas Advance Directives Act.
  • Medical Power of Attorney — Designates a person (the agent) to make medical decisions on behalf of the patient when the patient is unable to make or communicate decisions. Texas's Medical Power of Attorney is the primary surrogate decision-making document for families navigating hospice enrollment.
  • Out-of-Hospital Do-Not-Resuscitate Order (OOH-DNR) — Texas does not use the POLST (Physician Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment) form. Instead, Texas has the OOH-DNR, which is a physician-signed portable medical order directing emergency responders not to attempt resuscitation outside a hospital setting. This is especially important for hospice patients: without an OOH-DNR, EMS is legally required to attempt resuscitation even if the patient and family have expressed a wish to avoid it. Hospice intake nurses in San Antonio routinely help families complete or update the OOH-DNR as part of the enrollment process.

Hospice teams at San Antonio providers are experienced guiding families through these Texas-specific documents. If your family does not yet have a Medical Power of Attorney or OOH-DNR in place, raise this in the first conversation with any hospice provider — it is better to complete these documents before a medical crisis.

How to choose a hospice provider in San Antonio

Choosing a hospice provider is one of the most consequential decisions a family makes during an already difficult time. In San Antonio's competitive market, provider quality, cultural fit, and responsiveness vary meaningfully. Here is a practical framework:

  • Verify licensing and Medicare certification first. Confirm the provider holds a current Texas HHSC hospice license and is Medicare-certified. This is the baseline — do not enroll with an unlicensed provider.
  • Ask about after-hours nursing response. The most important quality indicator is how fast a nurse arrives at the bedside in a crisis — nights, weekends, holidays. Ask for a specific time commitment, not a general assurance. "We have 24/7 on-call coverage" means little without knowing response time.
  • Assess nurse-to-patient ratio. A smaller caseload per nurse means faster response and more attentive care. Ask how many patients each nurse currently manages and whether that is typical or reflects a staffing shortage.
  • Confirm geographic coverage for your ZIP code. San Antonio providers vary in how far they extend into Comal, Guadalupe, Medina, and other surrounding counties. Do not assume coverage — confirm it explicitly before enrolling.
  • Evaluate language and cultural fit. For Spanish-speaking families — especially multigenerational households common in San Antonio's South Side and West Side — confirm that bilingual nurses, social workers, and chaplains are available and not just reachable via a telephone interpreter service. In-person bilingual care is meaningfully different from interpreter-assisted care.
  • Understand insurance acceptance. Confirm the provider accepts your specific plan — Medicare, STAR+PLUS Medicaid, Medicare Advantage, TRICARE, or VA — before the intake visit. Ask whether they handle insurance verification before the first visit, which most reputable providers do.
  • Ask about inpatient respite and continuous care. Medicare's hospice benefit includes inpatient respite care (up to five days in a facility) and continuous home care during a medical crisis. Ask where the provider places patients for inpatient care and how quickly continuous care nursing can be arranged.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I find hospice care in San Antonio, TX?

Search BestHospice.com by ZIP code to find verified hospice providers serving San Antonio, New Braunfels, Schertz, Converse, Universal City, and across Bexar County. Free for families, no referral fees.

Does Medicare cover hospice in San Antonio?

Yes. Medicare Part A covers nursing visits, comfort medications, medical equipment, home health aide, social work, chaplain services, and bereavement counseling at little to no cost. A physician must certify a prognosis of six months or less. University Health, Baptist Health System, CHRISTUS Santa Rosa, and Methodist Healthcare physicians are experienced with this process.

Can veterans use hospice in San Antonio through the VA?

Yes. The South Texas Veterans Health Care System provides VA hospice benefits to eligible veterans, either through VA staff or contracted community providers. Veterans who are also Medicare-eligible can use community hospice under Medicare. Active-duty family members and retirees covered by TRICARE should ask providers about TRICARE coordination. Brooke Army Medical Center (BAMC) at Fort Sam Houston is a major military referral center for the region.

Does Texas STAR+PLUS Medicaid cover hospice in San Antonio?

Yes. STAR+PLUS covers hospice for qualifying low-income Bexar County residents. Managed care organizations in the area include Aetna Better Health of Texas, Humana Healthy Horizons, Molina Healthcare, Superior HealthPlan, and UnitedHealthcare Community Plan. Confirm STAR+PLUS interaction with the hospice benefit before enrolling.

Does Texas use POLST forms for hospice patients?

No. Texas does not use POLST. Texas uses the Out-of-Hospital Do-Not-Resuscitate Order (OOH-DNR) as its portable medical order for patients who do not want resuscitation outside a hospital. Texas also uses the Directive to Physicians, Family, or Surrogates (Texas Living Will) and the Medical Power of Attorney. San Antonio hospice teams help families complete these documents at enrollment.

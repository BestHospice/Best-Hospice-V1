Hospice Care in the Richmond Metro

Richmond is Virginia's capital and one of the Mid-Atlantic's most historically significant cities. The Richmond metro — spanning the independent City of Richmond, Chesterfield County, Henrico County, and the surrounding counties of Goochland, Powhatan, Hanover, and New Kent — has a well-developed hospice market anchored by strong academic and community health systems. The region's aging population and significant veteran community create consistent and sustained demand for quality hospice and palliative care services.

Richmond's Major Health Systems and Hospice Transitions

VCU Health (Virginia Commonwealth University Health System) is Virginia's only academic medical center in Richmond, with VCU Medical Center serving as the primary Level I trauma center and the Massey Comprehensive Cancer Center serving as an NCI-designated cancer research institution. VCU Health's palliative care and social work teams coordinate hospice transitions for complex oncology, cardiac, and multi-system patients from across Central Virginia.

Bon Secours Richmond Health System is the largest health system in the Richmond metro by number of hospitals, operating St. Mary's Hospital, Memorial Regional Medical Center, St. Francis Medical Center, and Richmond Community Hospital. Bon Secours' Catholic mission aligns naturally with hospice values of comfort and dignity, and their palliative care program coordinates end-of-life transitions across the system.

HCA Virginia Health System operates Chippenham Hospital (the region's largest private hospital), Johnston-Willis Hospital, and Henrico Doctors' Hospital across the metro's western and southern corridors. The Hunter Holmes McGuire VA Medical Center on Broad Rock Boulevard serves Richmond's significant veteran population and can coordinate hospice transitions for eligible veterans through the VA system.

Medicare and Virginia Medicaid Coverage in Richmond

Medicare Part A covers hospice for eligible Richmond-area patients with a physician-certified prognosis of six months or less. Covered services include nursing visits, comfort medications, medical equipment, home health aide, social work, chaplain services, and bereavement counseling — most at $0 to the patient or family.

Virginia Medicaid covers hospice through the Cardinal Care managed care program. Plans serving the Richmond area include Aetna Better Health of Virginia, Anthem HealthKeepers Plus, Molina Healthcare of Virginia, Optima Health Community Care, and UnitedHealthcare Community Plan of Virginia. Medicare Advantage plans from Anthem, Aetna, Humana, and UnitedHealthcare are widely available in the Richmond market — all must cover hospice at Medicare Part A levels.

Veterans enrolled in VA healthcare can access hospice through the McGuire VA Medical Center's palliative care program or through community hospice providers under VA's Community Care Network. Ask any Richmond hospice provider about veteran-specific programs, as many offer dedicated support for former service members and their families.

How to Choose a Hospice Provider in Richmond

  • Do they serve your specific community? Chesterfield, Henrico, and the City of Richmond each have different provider concentrations. Confirm active service in your county or ZIP code.
  • How quickly can they respond? Especially for hospital discharges from VCU Health, Bon Secours, or HCA Virginia facilities.
  • What is their experience with your loved one's diagnosis? Cancer, dementia, and cardiac disease each require different clinical expertise.
  • Are veteran-specific services available? If your loved one is a veteran, ask whether the provider has a We Honor Veterans certification or experience with veteran-specific emotional and spiritual needs.
  • Do they have an inpatient arrangement? Confirm where inpatient care would occur if symptoms escalate beyond what can be managed at home.

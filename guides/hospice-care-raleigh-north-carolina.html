Hospice Care in the Raleigh and Research Triangle Area

Raleigh is the capital of North Carolina and the anchor city of the Research Triangle — one of the fastest-growing metropolitan areas in the United States. The Triangle metro spans Wake, Durham, Orange, Chatham, Johnston, and surrounding counties, with Raleigh, Cary, Apex, Morrisville, Wake Forest, Garner, and Holly Springs among the fastest-growing communities. The region's rapid population growth — driven by tech industry expansion, university employment, and migration from across the country — has significantly increased demand for high-quality hospice and palliative care services.

BestHospice.com helps Raleigh-area families find partner hospice providers by ZIP code, compare services, and connect with local agencies without referral fees or sales pressure.

Raleigh's Major Health Systems and Hospice Transitions

UNC Health Rex (Rex Hospital) is the primary hospital for Raleigh and Wake County, affiliated with the University of North Carolina Health Care system. Rex's palliative care and social work teams coordinate hospice transitions for patients across Raleigh and the surrounding Wake County communities. UNC Hospitals in Chapel Hill — home to UNC Lineberger Comprehensive Cancer Center, an NCI-designated cancer center — is a major source of oncology hospice referrals for the broader Triangle region.

WakeMed Health is the other major hospital system in Wake County, operating WakeMed Raleigh Campus, WakeMed Cary Hospital, WakeMed North Healthplex, and other facilities across the county. WakeMed's palliative care program coordinates hospice transitions across its network. Duke Health, based in Durham, is one of the country's leading academic medical centers and a major source of complex oncology and cardiac hospice referrals for patients throughout the Triangle. Duke's Duke Cancer Institute is an NCI-designated Comprehensive Cancer Center.

Novant Health operates several hospitals and urgent care facilities in the greater Raleigh-Durham area as part of its expanding Triangle presence. The Triangle's large and growing population — with significant communities from India, East Asia, and Latin America tied to the region's tech and pharmaceutical sectors — means multilingual hospice services are increasingly important. Confirm language capabilities when evaluating providers.

Medicare and North Carolina Medicaid Coverage in Raleigh

Medicare Part A covers hospice for eligible Raleigh-area patients with a physician-certified prognosis of six months or less. Covered services include nursing visits, comfort medications, medical equipment, home health aide, social work, chaplain services, and bereavement counseling — most at $0 to the patient or family.

NC Medicaid Managed Care covers hospice for qualifying low-income North Carolina residents. Plans serving the Raleigh area include Aetna Better Health of NC, AmeriHealth Caritas NC, Carolina Complete Health (Centene), Healthy Blue (Blue Cross NC), UnitedHealthcare Community Plan of NC, and WellCare of NC. Medicare Advantage plans from Aetna, Blue Cross NC, UnitedHealthcare, Humana, and WellCare are widely available across the Triangle — all must cover hospice at Medicare Part A levels.

North Carolina licenses hospice providers through the NC Division of Health Service Regulation (DHSR). All Medicare-certified North Carolina hospice providers meet state licensing requirements as a condition of federal certification.

How to Choose a Hospice Provider in Raleigh

  • Do they serve your specific ZIP code? The Triangle's rapid growth means provider coverage zones vary. Cary, Apex, and Morrisville may be served by different agencies than central Raleigh.
  • How quickly can they respond? Especially for hospital discharges from UNC Health Rex, WakeMed, or Duke Health facilities in Durham.
  • What is their experience with your loved one's diagnosis? The Triangle's large oncology patient population means many providers have specialized cancer hospice experience.
  • What languages do they serve? The Triangle's diverse tech and university communities may need services in Hindi, Mandarin, Spanish, or other languages.
  • Do they have an inpatient arrangement? Confirm where inpatient care would occur if symptoms escalate beyond what can be managed at home.

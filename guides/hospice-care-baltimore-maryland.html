Hospice Care in Baltimore

Baltimore is Maryland's largest city and the anchor of one of the East Coast's most distinguished medical corridors. The greater Baltimore metro — encompassing Baltimore City, Baltimore County, Anne Arundel County (Annapolis), Howard County (Columbia, Ellicott City), Harford County, and Carroll County — is home to more than 2.9 million people and serves as the healthcare hub for much of central Maryland. Baltimore's nationally recognized academic medical centers generate a significant volume of complex oncology, cardiac, and neurological hospice referrals, making the city one of the most medically sophisticated hospice markets on the East Coast.

Baltimore's Major Health Systems

Johns Hopkins Medicine is one of the world's foremost academic medical institutions. Johns Hopkins Hospital — consistently ranked among the top hospitals in the United States — and Johns Hopkins Bayview Medical Center together serve as the primary referral centers for complex oncology, cardiac, and neurological cases throughout Maryland and the mid-Atlantic region. The Sidney Kimmel Comprehensive Cancer Center at Johns Hopkins is an NCI-designated Comprehensive Cancer Center and a major source of oncology hospice referrals. Johns Hopkins' palliative care program coordinates hospice transitions across its system.

University of Maryland Medical System (UMMS) includes the University of Maryland Medical Center (UMMC) in downtown Baltimore — home to the University of Maryland Marlene and Stewart Greenebaum Comprehensive Cancer Center — plus UM Baltimore Washington Medical Center, UM St. Joseph Medical Center, and numerous community hospitals across the state. UMMS's palliative care and social work teams coordinate hospice referrals across its network.

MedStar Health operates multiple Baltimore-area facilities including MedStar Franklin Square Medical Center, MedStar Good Samaritan Hospital, and MedStar Harbor Hospital, as well as MedStar Union Memorial Hospital. LifeBridge Health — including Sinai Hospital of Baltimore and Northwest Hospital — serves Baltimore City and northwest Baltimore County. Mercy Medical Center is a Catholic academic medical center in downtown Baltimore that also coordinates hospice transitions. The Baltimore VA Medical Center serves the region's veteran population.

Medicare and Maryland Medicaid HealthChoice Coverage

Medicare Part A covers hospice for eligible Baltimore-area patients with a physician-certified prognosis of six months or less. Covered services include nursing visits, comfort medications, medical equipment, home health aide, social work, chaplain services, and bereavement counseling — most at $0 to the patient or family.

Maryland Medicaid HealthChoice covers hospice for qualifying low-income Maryland residents. Plans serving the Baltimore metro include Aetna Better Health of Maryland, CareFirst Community Health Plan Maryland, Jai Medical Systems, MedStar Family Choice, Priority Partners (administered by Johns Hopkins), UnitedHealthcare Community Plan of Maryland, and Amerigroup Maryland. Dual-eligible patients can coordinate Medicare and Medicaid benefits. Medicare Advantage plans from CareFirst BlueCross BlueShield, Aetna, UnitedHealthcare, and Humana are widely available in the Baltimore market — all must cover hospice at Medicare Part A levels.

Maryland licenses hospice providers through the Maryland Department of Health. All Medicare-certified Maryland hospice providers meet state licensing requirements as a condition of federal certification.

How to Choose a Hospice Provider in Baltimore

  • Do they serve your specific community? Baltimore City, Baltimore County, Anne Arundel, Howard, and Harford counties may be served by different agencies. Confirm active service before scheduling intake.
  • How quickly can they respond? Especially for hospital discharges from Johns Hopkins, UMMC, or MedStar facilities.
  • What is their experience with your loved one's diagnosis? Johns Hopkins and UMMC generate a large volume of oncology referrals — many Baltimore hospices have deep cancer care experience.
  • Does the hospice have experience with veterans? The Baltimore VA Medical Center serves a significant veteran population in the metro area. Ask whether the provider has VA partnerships or veteran-specific experience.
  • Do they have an inpatient arrangement? Confirm where inpatient care would occur if symptoms escalate beyond what can be managed at home.

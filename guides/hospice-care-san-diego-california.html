Hospice care in San Diego

San Diego is the second-largest city in California and the eighth-largest in the United States, situated in the southwestern corner of the country at the US-Mexico border. San Diego County has a large and growing senior population, a major military and veteran community tied to multiple Navy and Marine Corps installations, and a significant cross-border population — many San Diego residents maintain healthcare relationships on both sides of the US-Mexico border. The hospice market in San Diego is well-developed, with providers serving urban neighborhoods, inland communities, and coastal areas from the South Bay to the North County coast.

San Diego County's geography spans diverse communities with different service access considerations. Families in the South Bay (Chula Vista, National City, San Ysidro) are close to the US-Mexico border and often prefer Spanish-speaking providers. East County communities (El Cajon, Santee, La Mesa, Alpine) may have different provider options than coastal or inland North County (Escondido, Vista, San Marcos, Carlsbad, Encinitas, Oceanside). Always confirm coverage by ZIP code before enrolling.

San Diego's large active-duty military and veteran population makes VA hospice benefits a particularly important consideration in this market. Unlike in most other major US cities, a substantial portion of San Diego families evaluating hospice options for a loved one need to understand how VA benefits interact with or complement Medicare hospice — and whether the hospice provider has experience working with the VA system.

California licenses hospice providers through the California Department of Public Health (CDPH), Licensing and Certification Division. All Medicare-certified hospice providers in California must meet CDPH licensing requirements as a condition of federal certification. California does not require a Certificate of Need (CON) for hospice licensure — any provider meeting state licensing and Medicare certification requirements may operate in the state.

Major San Diego health systems and hospice transitions

San Diego County is served by several major health systems, each with palliative care programs and social work teams that coordinate hospice referrals. Families can also contact a hospice provider directly — most San Diego hospice agencies will work with any of these institutions to receive records and facilitate a smooth transition.

  • UC San Diego Health (La Jolla / Hillcrest) — The primary academic medical center for San Diego County, with Jacobs Medical Center and the Moores Cancer Center in La Jolla, an NCI-designated Comprehensive Cancer Center. UCSD's palliative care program coordinates hospice transitions for complex oncology cases and other serious illness across the region. The UCSD Health team at Hillcrest also serves patients in central San Diego. UCSD social workers can facilitate referrals to community hospice providers throughout the county.
  • Sharp HealthCare — The largest private healthcare system in San Diego County, operating Sharp Memorial Hospital (Kearny Mesa), Sharp Chula Vista Medical Center (South Bay), Sharp Grossmont Hospital (El Cajon / East County), and Sharp Coronado Hospital. Sharp also operates a regional hospice and palliative care program. Patients transitioning out of any Sharp facility can work with the Sharp palliative care or case management team to facilitate a community hospice referral or transition to Sharp's own hospice program.
  • Scripps Health — Operates Scripps Mercy Hospital (Hillcrest and Chula Vista campuses), Scripps Memorial Hospital La Jolla, Scripps Green Hospital (La Jolla), and Scripps Encinitas Hospital across coastal and inland San Diego communities. Each Scripps campus has palliative care and case management resources. Scripps serves a broad geographic area of San Diego County, from North County coastal communities to the South Bay.
  • Rady Children's Hospital (San Diego) — The region's pediatric referral center and home to one of Southern California's leading pediatric palliative care programs. For families of children with life-limiting illness, Rady Children's palliative care team coordinates pediatric hospice transitions to community providers with pediatric hospice experience.
  • VA San Diego Healthcare System (La Jolla) — Serves the large military veteran population in San Diego County. Veterans who are enrolled in VA health care and qualify for hospice may receive care through the VA's own inpatient or home hospice program, or through a VA-contracted community hospice provider. See the Medicare, Medi-Cal & VA section below for detail on how VA benefits work alongside Medicare.
  • Naval Medical Center San Diego / Balboa Hospital (Balboa Park area) — The largest military treatment facility on the West Coast and a major source of referrals for active-duty service members and military retirees and their families. Families with active-duty or TRICARE-covered loved ones should confirm their TRICARE plan's hospice coverage and the provider's TRICARE contract status when evaluating options.

When transitioning from any San Diego hospital, ask to speak with the palliative care team or inpatient social worker. They can initiate a referral, transfer medical records, and coordinate discharge timing with the hospice provider — often enabling a same-day or next-day start after discharge.

Medicare, Medi-Cal, and VA hospice coverage in San Diego

Medicare Part A

Medicare Part A covers hospice comprehensively for eligible San Diego patients in 2026. To qualify, a physician must certify a terminal prognosis of six months or less if the illness runs its normal course. Covered services include:

  • Nursing visits — $0 to the patient
  • Comfort-focused medications for the terminal diagnosis — $0 or up to $5 per prescription
  • Medical equipment (hospital bed, oxygen, wheelchair, commode) — $0
  • Home health aide and homemaker services — $0
  • Social worker, chaplain, and bereavement counseling — $0
  • Inpatient respite care — 5% coinsurance
  • Continuous home care during a medical crisis — $0 or minimal cost

Medicare Advantage plans available in San Diego — including those offered by UnitedHealthcare, Humana, Aetna, Blue Shield of California, Kaiser Permanente, Sharp Health Plan, and Community Health Group — are required to cover hospice at the same levels as traditional Medicare Part A. Verify your specific plan's hospice network with the hospice provider before enrolling.

Medi-Cal (California Medicaid)

Medi-Cal covers hospice for qualifying low-income California residents. In San Diego County, Medi-Cal managed care plans include:

  • Molina Healthcare
  • Health Net
  • Community Health Group
  • Kaiser Permanente
  • Blue Shield Promise Health Plan

Ask your hospice provider to verify your specific Medi-Cal managed care plan and confirm they are contracted with it before enrollment. For patients enrolled in both Medicare and Medi-Cal (dual eligibles), Medicare is the primary payer for hospice and Medi-Cal may cover certain additional costs. California's IHSS (In-Home Supportive Services) program may also be available to qualifying patients — confirm with the hospice provider how IHSS interacts with the hospice benefit, as the two programs can overlap in ways that require coordination.

VA hospice benefits for San Diego veterans

San Diego County has one of the largest veteran populations of any county in the United States, tied to Naval Air Station North Island, Naval Base San Diego, Marine Corps Air Station Miramar, Camp Pendleton, and multiple other installations. Veterans who are enrolled in VA health care may be eligible for hospice through the VA San Diego Healthcare System — either through VA-provided care or a VA-contracted community hospice agency.

Key facts about VA hospice benefits in San Diego:

  • VA hospice benefits are separate from Medicare hospice benefits. A veteran may use both simultaneously if eligible for both, with each covering different services or periods of care.
  • Veterans do not have to forgo VA benefits to receive Medicare hospice — but coordination between the VA and the community hospice provider is essential.
  • Ask prospective hospice providers whether they hold a VA contract and have experience billing the VA and coordinating with VA care managers in San Diego.
  • Veterans with service-connected disabilities may receive additional VA benefits even while on hospice. Ask the VA social worker or patient advocate about all available benefits.
  • The VA San Diego Healthcare System has its own palliative care program at its La Jolla campus. The VA social worker or palliative care team can help determine the best hospice pathway for a veteran patient.

PACE in San Diego

California has an extensive PACE (Program of All-inclusive Care for the Elderly) network, with PACE sites operating in San Diego. If a patient is currently enrolled in PACE and transitions to hospice, PACE enrollment will need to be adjusted. Coordinate these changes carefully with both the PACE program and the hospice provider to ensure continuity of care and benefits.

Free Medicare counseling: HICAP

California's SHIP program is called HICAP — the Health Insurance Counseling and Advocacy Program — administered through county Area Agencies on Aging and the California Department of Aging. HICAP provides free, unbiased Medicare counseling on hospice benefits, Medi-Cal interaction with Medicare hospice, Medicare Advantage plan questions, and more. In San Diego, HICAP is administered through the San Diego County Aging & Independence Services office. Call 1-800-434-0222 or visit the California Department of Aging website to find your local HICAP counselor.

California laws, advance directives, and end-of-life options

Advance Health Care Directive (AHCD)

California uses the Advance Health Care Directive (AHCD) to allow individuals to document their end-of-life care preferences and designate a healthcare agent (also called a healthcare proxy or power of attorney for healthcare). The AHCD becomes effective when an individual lacks the capacity to make their own decisions. Hospice social workers and admissions staff can help families complete or update an AHCD, and copies should be provided to the hospice provider, primary physician, and any hospitals where the patient may be treated.

POLST (Physician Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment)

California uses the POLST form — called POLST in California — as a portable medical order for patients with serious illness or advanced age. Unlike an advance directive (which documents preferences), a POLST is a physician-signed medical order that can be honored by emergency responders, paramedics, and healthcare providers across care settings. Hospice patients are strongly encouraged to have a signed POLST displayed prominently in the home. The hospice nurse or physician can assist with completing and signing the POLST form during the enrollment process.

California End of Life Option Act

California's End of Life Option Act (effective June 2016, amended 2021) allows terminally ill California adults with a prognosis of six months or less to request a prescription for aid-in-dying medication from a participating physician. This option is entirely separate from hospice — patients can be enrolled in hospice and separately inquire about the End of Life Option Act with their physician. Not all physicians or hospice organizations participate; if this option is important to your family, raise it during your evaluation of hospice providers and confirm whether the organization and its affiliated physicians participate.

Hospice licensure: no CON requirement

California does not require a Certificate of Need (CON) for hospice licensure. Any provider meeting California Department of Public Health (CDPH) Licensing and Certification Division requirements and Medicare certification standards may operate in the state. This open-entry regulatory environment means San Diego has a robust hospice market with multiple competing providers — giving families real choices but also requiring careful evaluation.

How to choose a hospice provider in San Diego

San Diego has a well-developed hospice market with providers serving communities from the South Bay to the North County, and from the coast to the inland desert. Here are the most important factors to evaluate when choosing a provider:

  • Coverage by ZIP code and community. San Diego County is geographically large and diverse. Confirm the provider serves your specific community — whether you are in Chula Vista, Escondido, Oceanside, El Cajon, La Jolla, Encinitas, or another area. Do not assume coverage based on a general San Diego service area claim.
  • VA experience and contract status. If your loved one is a veteran, ask whether the hospice provider holds a current VA contract and has experience coordinating with the VA San Diego Healthcare System. Coordination between VA benefits and Medicare hospice requires a provider with specific experience; not all providers have it.
  • Spanish-language services. The South Bay communities of Chula Vista, National City, and San Ysidro have large Hispanic/Latino populations. Many hospice providers in the South Bay offer services entirely in Spanish. If Spanish is the primary language in your household, confirm that bilingual staff — not just interpreter services — are available for nursing visits, family meetings, and social work support.
  • After-hours response. Ask specifically about after-hours nursing coverage: Is a nurse available by phone 24/7? What is the typical response time for an urgent in-person visit in your area of San Diego County? Response times and availability can vary across communities, especially in more rural parts of East County or far North County.
  • Insurance acceptance. Confirm the provider accepts your specific insurance — Medicare, your Medicare Advantage plan, your Medi-Cal managed care plan (Molina, Health Net, Community Health Group, Kaiser, Blue Shield Promise), or VA benefits. Not all providers are contracted with all plans.
  • Diagnosis-specific experience. Ask whether the clinical team has experience with your loved one's primary diagnosis — cancer, heart failure, COPD, dementia, ALS, or other conditions. Clinical depth in the relevant disease area leads to better symptom management and anticipatory guidance for families.
  • Inpatient hospice access. Ask whether the provider has access to a contracted inpatient hospice unit or GIP (General Inpatient) level care in San Diego County for symptom management crises that cannot be managed at home. Know the location of this unit before a crisis arises.
  • Bereavement and volunteer services. All California-licensed hospice agencies are required to provide bereavement counseling to surviving family members. Ask about the scope and duration of bereavement support and whether the provider offers volunteer services such as companionship visits or caregiver respite.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I find hospice care in San Diego?

Search BestHospice.com by ZIP code to find partner hospice providers serving San Diego, Chula Vista, El Cajon, Escondido, Oceanside, Encinitas, La Jolla, and communities across San Diego County. Coverage areas vary — always confirm your specific ZIP code with the provider before enrolling.

Does Medicare cover hospice in San Diego?

Yes. Medicare Part A covers nursing visits, comfort medications, medical equipment, home health aide, social work, chaplain services, and bereavement counseling at little to no out-of-pocket cost in 2026. A physician must certify a prognosis of six months or less. Medicare Advantage plans in San Diego must cover hospice at the same levels as traditional Medicare.

Does Medi-Cal cover hospice in San Diego?

Yes. Medi-Cal covers hospice for qualifying low-income California residents. In San Diego County, Medi-Cal managed care plans include Molina Healthcare, Health Net, Community Health Group, Kaiser Permanente, and Blue Shield Promise Health Plan. Ask your provider to verify your specific plan before enrolling. IHSS recipients should confirm how that program interacts with the hospice benefit.

Can veterans use VA hospice benefits in San Diego?

Yes. Veterans enrolled in VA health care who qualify for hospice may receive care through the VA San Diego Healthcare System or a VA-contracted community hospice provider. VA hospice benefits are separate from Medicare — veterans may be eligible to use both simultaneously. Ask prospective providers about their VA contract status and experience coordinating VA benefits. The VA San Diego palliative care team can help determine the best hospice pathway.

How do I transition from UCSD, Sharp, or Scripps to hospice in San Diego?

All three health systems have palliative care teams and social workers who facilitate hospice referrals. Ask your physician or the hospital social worker to request a palliative care consultation. You can also contact a San Diego hospice provider directly — they can receive records from UCSD, Sharp, or Scripps and begin the intake process before discharge, often enabling a same-day or next-day start after leaving the hospital.

What advance directive forms does California use?

California uses the Advance Health Care Directive (AHCD) to document end-of-life wishes and designate a healthcare agent. California also uses the POLST form as a portable medical order that emergency responders and care providers can follow. Hospice teams can help complete or update both documents. California's End of Life Option Act separately allows terminally ill adults to request aid-in-dying medication from a participating physician — separate from hospice, with not all providers participating.

Do San Diego hospice providers offer Spanish-language services?

Many do, especially in South Bay communities like Chula Vista, National City, and San Ysidro. Ask prospective providers whether they have bilingual Spanish-speaking staff available for nursing visits, social work, and family meetings — not just telephone interpreter services. Spanish-language care is widely available in San Diego's South Bay hospice market.

What is HICAP and how can it help my family?

HICAP — the Health Insurance Counseling and Advocacy Program — is California's free Medicare counseling program. HICAP counselors provide unbiased guidance on hospice benefits, how Medi-Cal interacts with Medicare hospice, VA benefit questions, and Medicare Advantage plan options. In San Diego, HICAP is administered through San Diego County Aging & Independence Services. Call 1-800-434-0222 to connect with your local HICAP counselor.

How quickly can hospice start in San Diego?

Most San Diego hospice providers can begin within 24 to 48 hours. Same-day starts are often possible for urgent discharge situations from UCSD Health, Sharp Memorial, Scripps Mercy, or other San Diego hospitals. The provider handles physician certification and insurance verification before the first visit.

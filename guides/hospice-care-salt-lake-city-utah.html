Hospice Care in Salt Lake City and the Wasatch Front

Salt Lake City sits at the center of the Wasatch Front, the urban corridor stretching from Ogden in the north through Salt Lake County to the Point of the Mountain, which is home to more than 80% of Utah's population. The Salt Lake metro — including Salt Lake City, West Valley City, Murray, Millcreek, South Salt Lake, Sandy, Cottonwood Heights, Holladay, and the communities of south Salt Lake County — has a well-developed hospice market anchored by two major health systems that dominate healthcare across the Mountain West.

Utah's strong family culture means many families prefer to care for loved ones at home as long as possible. Hospice providers in the Salt Lake area are experienced with this preference and are well-equipped to support family caregivers with frequent nursing visits, aide services, and 24/7 on-call access.

Salt Lake City's Major Health Systems and Hospice Transitions

University of Utah Health is Utah's academic medical center and one of the Mountain West's premier academic health systems. University of Utah Hospital and Clinics, along with Huntsman Cancer Institute — an NCI-designated Comprehensive Cancer Center — are the primary referral centers for complex oncology, cardiac, and neurological cases across Utah and the surrounding region. The University of Utah's palliative care program coordinates hospice transitions for patients completing active treatment at Huntsman and across the university system.

Intermountain Health (formerly Intermountain Healthcare) is the largest nonprofit health system in the Mountain West and the dominant community hospital network along the Wasatch Front. Key facilities include Intermountain Medical Center in Murray (the flagship), LDS Hospital in Salt Lake City, Alta View Hospital in Sandy, and Riverton Hospital in Riverton. Intermountain's palliative care and case management teams coordinate hospice referrals across its extensive network of hospitals and clinics.

The Salt Lake area also has a significant refugee and immigrant population, with large communities from Southeast Asia, Latin America, and East Africa. Hospice providers serving the urban core of Salt Lake City may offer multilingual services — confirm language capabilities when evaluating providers.

Medicare and Utah Medicaid Coverage in Salt Lake City

Medicare Part A covers hospice for eligible Salt Lake City-area patients with a physician-certified prognosis of six months or less. Covered services include nursing visits, comfort medications, medical equipment, home health aide, social work, chaplain services, and bereavement counseling — most at $0 to the patient or family.

Utah Medicaid covers hospice for qualifying low-income residents. Managed care plans include Healthy U (University of Utah Health Plans), Molina Healthcare of Utah, and SelectHealth (Intermountain). Dual-eligible patients can coordinate Medicare and Medicaid benefits. Medicare Advantage plans from SelectHealth, UnitedHealthcare, Humana, and Aetna are widely available in the Salt Lake market — all must cover hospice at Medicare Part A levels.

Utah licenses hospice providers through the Utah Department of Health and Human Services. All Medicare-certified Utah hospice providers meet state licensing requirements as a condition of federal certification.

How to Choose a Hospice Provider in Salt Lake City

  • Do they serve your specific community? Murray, Sandy, West Valley City, and Millcreek all have different provider coverage. Confirm active service before scheduling intake.
  • How quickly can they respond? Especially for hospital discharges from University of Utah or Intermountain facilities.
  • What is their experience with your loved one's diagnosis? Oncology patients from Huntsman Cancer Institute, cardiac patients from Intermountain, and dementia patients all have different care needs.
  • Do they support family caregiver involvement? Utah families often have large, active caregiver networks. Ask how the hospice team integrates with family caregivers.
  • Do they have an inpatient arrangement? Confirm where inpatient care would be provided if symptoms escalate beyond what can be managed at home.

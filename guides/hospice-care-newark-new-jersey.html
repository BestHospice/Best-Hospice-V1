Hospice Care in Newark and Northern New Jersey

Newark is New Jersey's largest city and the anchor of the Northern New Jersey urban corridor — one of the most densely populated and diverse regions in the United States. The greater Newark area — spanning Essex, Hudson, Union, Bergen, and Passaic counties — is home to more than 2.5 million people and sits immediately adjacent to New York City. The region's proximity to Manhattan, combined with its own network of major medical centers, gives Newark-area families access to a well-developed hospice market.

Northern New Jersey's extraordinary diversity — with large Puerto Rican, Dominican, Ecuadorian, Haitian, Brazilian, Indian, and many other immigrant communities — means hospice providers must often deliver care in Spanish, Haitian Creole, Portuguese, Hindi, and other languages. Confirm language capabilities early when evaluating providers, particularly for families in Newark, Jersey City, Elizabeth, and Paterson.

Newark's Major Health Systems and Hospice Transitions

RWJBarnabas Health is the largest health system in New Jersey, operating Newark Beth Israel Medical Center (a major cardiac center and one of the busiest hospitals in the state), Clara Maass Medical Center in Belleville, Saint Barnabas Medical Center in Livingston, and numerous other facilities across New Jersey. RWJBarnabas Health's palliative care and social work teams coordinate hospice transitions across its extensive network. Hackensack Meridian Health is New Jersey's largest integrated health network, operating Hackensack University Medical Center (the flagship, with John Theurer Cancer Center — an NCI-designated cancer program), Jersey City Medical Center, and facilities across Bergen, Hudson, Middlesex, and Monmouth counties. Hackensack's palliative care program coordinates hospice referrals for patients across Northern and Central New Jersey.

University Hospital Newark — New Jersey's only Level 1 trauma center and academic medical center — is affiliated with Rutgers New Jersey Medical School and serves as the state's primary safety-net hospital for complex cases. University Hospital's social work and palliative care teams support hospice transitions for Newark and Essex County patients. Atlantic Health System includes Morristown Medical Center (with the Carol G. Simon Cancer Center), Overlook Medical Center in Summit, Newton Medical Center, and Chilton Medical Center — and coordinates hospice referrals across Morris, Union, and Sussex counties.

Medicare and NJ FamilyCare Coverage in Newark

Medicare Part A covers hospice for eligible Northern New Jersey patients with a physician-certified prognosis of six months or less. Covered services include nursing visits, comfort medications, medical equipment, home health aide, social work, chaplain services, and bereavement counseling — most at $0 to the patient or family.

NJ FamilyCare (New Jersey's Medicaid program) covers hospice for qualifying low-income New Jersey residents. Plans serving Northern New Jersey include Aetna Better Health of New Jersey, Horizon NJ Health (Blue Cross Blue Shield of NJ), UnitedHealthcare Community Plan of New Jersey, WellCare of New Jersey, and AmeriHealth Caritas New Jersey. Dual-eligible patients can coordinate Medicare and Medicaid benefits. Medicare Advantage plans from Aetna, Horizon Blue Cross Blue Shield, UnitedHealthcare, and Humana are widely available across Northern New Jersey — all must cover hospice at Medicare Part A levels.

New Jersey licenses hospice providers through the New Jersey Department of Health. All Medicare-certified New Jersey hospice providers meet state licensing requirements as a condition of federal certification.

How to Choose a Hospice Provider in Newark

  • Do they serve your specific county and community? Essex, Hudson, Union, Bergen, and Passaic counties may be served by different agencies. Confirm active service before scheduling intake.
  • What languages do they serve? Spanish is widely spoken across Essex, Hudson, and Union counties. Confirm language capability early.
  • How quickly can they respond? Especially for hospital discharges from Newark Beth Israel, Hackensack University Medical Center, University Hospital, or Morristown Medical Center.
  • What is their experience with your loved one's diagnosis? John Theurer Cancer Center at Hackensack and Carol G. Simon Cancer Center at Morristown generate significant oncology referrals. Newark Beth Israel is a major cardiac referral source.
  • Do they have an inpatient arrangement? Confirm where inpatient hospice would occur if symptoms escalate beyond what can be managed at home.

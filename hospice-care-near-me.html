What to Expect When You Find Hospice Care Near You

Once you identify a hospice provider near you, the enrollment process typically works as follows:

  • Initial call: You contact the hospice provider. They ask about your loved one's diagnosis, current care setting (home, hospital, nursing facility), and insurance.
  • Physician certification: The hospice provider works with your loved one's physician to complete the terminal prognosis certification required by Medicare and most insurance plans.
  • First visit: A registered nurse visits to complete a comprehensive assessment and establish the care plan. Most providers can do this within 24–48 hours of first contact.
  • Ongoing care: Nursing visits, medications, equipment, aide services, social work, and chaplain visits begin based on the care plan. Family members receive bereavement support.

You can change hospice providers at any time if your needs change or you are unsatisfied with care. A provider change does not affect Medicare hospice benefits.

What Medicare Covers for Hospice Care Near You

Medicare Part A covers hospice for eligible patients with a physician-certified prognosis of six months or less. In 2026, covered services include:

  • Nursing visits (registered nurse and licensed practical nurse)
  • Comfort medications related to the terminal diagnosis
  • Medical equipment: hospital bed, wheelchair, oxygen, walker
  • Home health aide services (bathing, personal care)
  • Social work counseling and family support
  • Chaplain and spiritual care services
  • Bereavement counseling for family members after the patient's death

Most hospice services are covered at $0 to the patient or family under Medicare Part A. Medicare Advantage plans must cover hospice at the same level as traditional Medicare. See the full Medicare hospice coverage guide →

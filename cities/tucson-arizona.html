Finding Hospice Care in Tucson, AZ

Tucson is Arizona's second-largest city and home to a well-established hospice and home care community with deep roots in the area. The Tucson metro — including Marana, Oro Valley, Sahuarita, and Green Valley — supports multiple hospice agencies that serve both urban neighborhoods and the sprawling suburban and rural communities south and northwest of the city.

Major health systems serving Tucson include Banner Health (operating Banner – University Medical Center Tucson), Carondelet Health Network (Carondelet St. Mary's and St. Joseph's hospitals, part of CommonSpirit Health), and Tucson Medical Center (TMC). The University of Arizona's health sciences campus has strong palliative care programming. Tucson also has a significant veteran population served by the Southern Arizona VA Health Care System, which can coordinate hospice alongside VA benefits.

Medicare and AHCCCS Coverage in Tucson

Medicare Part A covers hospice for eligible Tucson patients at little to no out-of-pocket cost when a physician certifies a terminal prognosis of six months or less. Covered services include nursing visits, comfort medications, home-delivered medical equipment, aide services, social work, chaplain services, and bereavement counseling.

Arizona's AHCCCS Medicaid program and the ALTCS long-term care program cover hospice for qualifying low-income Tucson residents. Dual-eligible patients (Medicare + AHCCCS) typically have all hospice costs covered. Arizona has no Certificate of Need requirement, so Tucson families have a meaningful range of hospice agency options.

Veterans enrolled in VA healthcare may also receive palliative care and hospice support through the Southern Arizona VA Health Care System. Ask a VA social worker or private hospice provider about how VA and Medicare benefits can coordinate.

Choosing Hospice Care in Tucson

Southern Arizona has a strong tradition of in-home care — many Tucson families prioritize keeping their loved one at home rather than in a facility. When comparing providers, ask how frequently a nurse will visit, what after-hours on-call coverage looks like, and whether the team has experience with your loved one's specific diagnosis.

Common qualifying diagnoses for hospice in Tucson include advanced cancer, end-stage heart failure, advanced COPD, late-stage Alzheimer's or dementia, kidney failure, ALS, and advanced Parkinson's disease. Any provider can conduct a free eligibility review before enrollment.

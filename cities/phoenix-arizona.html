Finding Hospice Care in Phoenix, AZ

Phoenix is the fifth-largest city in the United States and the center of one of the country's fastest-growing metro areas. The Valley of the Sun — encompassing Scottsdale, Tempe, Mesa, Chandler, Gilbert, Glendale, Peoria, Surprise, and surrounding communities — is home to a large and aging population with strong demand for quality hospice and home care.

Major health systems serving Phoenix include Banner Health, HonorHealth, Dignity Health (now CommonSpirit Health), and Mayo Clinic Arizona. These systems operate hospital-based palliative and hospice consultation programs, while independent hospice agencies deliver the majority of in-home hospice care across the metro area. For most Phoenix families, in-home hospice is the preferred option — it allows patients to remain in familiar surroundings near family.

Medicare and AHCCCS Coverage in Phoenix

Medicare Part A covers hospice care for eligible Phoenix patients at little to no out-of-pocket cost when a physician certifies a terminal prognosis of six months or less. Covered services include nursing visits, comfort medications related to the terminal diagnosis, medical equipment delivered to the home, home health aide support, social work, chaplain services, and bereavement counseling.

Arizona's Medicaid program — AHCCCS (Arizona Health Care Cost Containment System) — also covers hospice for qualifying low-income residents. The ALTCS (Arizona Long-Term Care System) program, administered through AHCCCS, can coordinate supplemental long-term care benefits for dual-eligible patients. Patients eligible for both Medicare and AHCCCS typically have all hospice costs covered.

Medicare Advantage plan enrollees in Phoenix — through BlueCross BlueShield of Arizona, UnitedHealthcare, Humana, Aetna, and others — are required to receive the same hospice benefits as traditional Medicare. Confirm in-network hospice providers with your specific plan before enrolling.

Choosing a Hospice Provider in Phoenix

When comparing hospice agencies in Phoenix, ask each provider: how quickly can intake begin, is a live nurse available by phone at 2 a.m., how often will a nurse visit the home, and what experience does the team have with your loved one's specific diagnosis. Strong providers respond fast, communicate clearly, and coordinate directly with your loved one's physician.

Phoenix has many hospice agencies — from large national organizations to smaller locally owned teams. Size alone is not a predictor of quality. A smaller local agency may offer more personalized attention; a larger system may have more staffing redundancy. Ask for references and read available quality data on Medicare Care Compare before making a final decision.
